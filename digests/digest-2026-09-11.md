## Journal of Computer Assisted Learning (2편)

### 1. Generative AI and Learning Dynamics in Higher Education: A Longitudinal Empirical Study
- 저자: Chrysanthi Melanou, Maik Beege, Martin Kimmig  |  게재: 2026-09-10
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70322](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70322)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Generative AI tools such as ChatGPT are increasingly integrated into higher education. Empirical evidence on how they shape learning dynamics over time remains limited. Previous studies have often examined single learning outcomes, neglecting the interplay between cognitive, motivational, and metacognitive processes that evolve throughout the semester. Objectives This longitudinal study investigated how generative AI influences students' knowledge gain, motivation, cognitive load, critical thinking, and reflective use across an entire semester. It further explored whether prior knowledge and AI experience moderated these learning effects, addressing concerns about potential Matthew effects. Methods A quasi‐experimental design was implemented with three parallel classes of business informatics students ( N = 87) assigned to different instructional conditions: tutor‐AI use, unguided AI, and a control group without AI. Data were collected at three measurement points (T1–T3). Results and Conclusion Students showed significant knowledge gains across all groups, but no additional benefits of AI integration. Motivation remained largely stable; germane cognitive load increased and was positively associated with knowledge gain. Critical thinking remained stable, while reflective use was higher in the AI condition and positively associated with critical thinking. No evidence emerged for a Matthew effect, indicating that AI did not intensify performance inequalities. Overall, the findings did not support the view of generative AI as a consistent learning outcome. Its impact depended on pedagogical framing and learners' metacognitive regulation, underscoring the need for structured guidance and reflective engagement in AI‐supported education.

### 2. The Great Debaters Meet the Great Facilitators: How Debate Students Evaluate Human Moderators and Their AI Counterparts
- 저자: Mohammad Amin Kuhail, Butros M. Dahu, Javeed Kittur, Erik Thornquist  |  게재: 2026-09-10
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70319](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70319)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Debate cultivates reasoning, logic and persuasion, but its impact depends on skilled moderation, a role traditionally reserved for humans. Advances in large language models (LLMs) make Artificial Intelligence (AI) moderators a plausible alternative. Yet, little is known about how students perceive AI moderators compared to their human counterparts in educational debates. Objectives This study examines how university students evaluate human versus AI moderation in a synchronous, text‐based debate platform, focusing on moderation effectiveness, enjoyment, satisfaction, perceived utility and intention to use. Methods A quantitative experimental study was conducted using a synchronous text‐based debate platform. The study involved 32 university students from various universities in the United States who participated in 32 debate sessions. Each participant engaged in two debates moderated by human moderators, AI moderators or a combination of both across four moderator conditions: Human–Human, Bot–Bot, Human–Bot and Bot–Human. Following each debate, participants completed a questionnaire assessing moderation effectiveness, hedonic motivation, satisfaction, perceived utility and usage intention, resulting in a total of 64 observations. Data were analysed using descriptive statistics, independent‐sample t ‐tests, multiple regression analyses and mediation analyses to examine differences between moderator types and the relationships among the study constructs. Ethical approval was obtained from the university's Research Ethics Committee, and informed consent was secured from all participants. Results and Conclusions Our analysis reveals no significant differences between human‐ and AI‐moderated debates across all measured dimensions. Across conditions, perceived effectiveness of moderation and hedonic motivation to use the platform predicted satisfaction and perceived utility. Perceived utility significantly drove usage intention, especially under AI moderation, whereas satisfaction more strongly predicted usage intention with human moderators. These findings suggest that, in well‐scaffolded, text‐based contexts with clear rules, thoughtfully designed AI moderators can match human facilitation while offering scalable support for equitable participation and structured discourse.
