## Journal of Computer Assisted Learning (2편)

### 1. Multimedia Learning Outcomes and Cognitive Load: The Roles of Spatial Ability, Enjoyment, Domain, and Resource Type
- 저자: Natalie Toomey, Misook Heo  |  게재: 2026-10-01
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70345](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70345)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Multimedia learning research has primarily emphasized cognitive predictors of learning, with less attention to affective engagement despite its potential relevance to effort allocation during learning tasks. Objectives This study examined the joint predictive associations of spatial ability, enjoyment, gender, and multimedia type (static versus animated) with learning outcomes and perceived cognitive load in parallel across two tasks (biology and mechanics). Methods Voluntary online participation was completed by 111 university students who were randomly assigned to static or animated resources. Participants completed a spatial ability measure (Paper Folding Test), two multimedia learning tasks, post‐task enjoyment ratings, and a cognitive load questionnaire assessing intrinsic, extraneous, and germane load. Multivariate multiple linear regression was used to model learning outcomes and cognitive load within each task. Results and Conclusions Across both domains, higher spatial ability predicted higher learning performance and lower perceived extraneous cognitive load. Enjoyment was positively associated with germane cognitive load in both tasks, with additional task‐specific associations with intrinsic and extraneous load. Multimedia type and gender did not significantly predict learning outcomes or cognitive load in either task. This study contributes to the literature by examining cognitive (spatial ability) and affective (enjoyment) learner characteristics jointly along with multimedia type, while modelling learning outcomes and cognitive load as complementary parallel outcomes. Through this approach, this study clarified the distinct roles of cognitive and affective learner characteristics when static and animated materials are instructionally equivalent. Specifically, it identified spatial ability as a reliable individual‐difference factor associated with more efficient multimedia processing and found that enjoyment was associated with higher reported germane cognitive load. These findings highlight the importance of considering both cognitive capacity and task‐level engagement in multimedia learning design.

### 2. Which Evaluation Strategies Help Students Spot Visual Misinformation on Social Media?
- 저자: Shai Goldfarb Cohen, Sarit Barzilai, Linor Lea Hadar, Thuraia Copti-Mshael, Liron Primor  |  게재: 2026-10-01
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70344](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70344)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background The rapid spread of visual misinformation online, intensified by advances in generative artificial intelligence, presents new challenges for adolescents' digital information literacy. Although students' difficulties in evaluating online information are well documented, little is known about how they assess different forms of visual misinformation on social media. Objectives This study examined the extent to which lower secondary school students can successfully identify different types of visual misinformation on social media, the evaluation strategies they use, and whether these strategies predict misinformation detection across types of visual misinformation. Methods The participants were 272 students in Grades 7–9 who evaluated social media posts containing visual misinformation, including manipulated videos, manipulated images, and AI‐generated images. Students rated each item's credibility and provided written justifications. Evaluation strategies were coded using a theoretically grounded scheme. Results Students were moderately successful in identifying manipulated images and videos, but struggled to detect AI‐generated images, which were often perceived as credible. Students relied primarily on evaluation of visual appearance and content, while source evaluation and corroboration strategies were infrequently used. Across all visual misinformation types, examination of visual features, knowledge‐based evaluation, sourcing, and corroboration strategies predicted misinformation detection. However, corroboration was the only strategy that reliably predicted identification of AI‐generated images. Conclusions Students mostly rely on first‐hand evaluation of visual appearance and content, but these strategies are inadequate for detecting realistic AI‐generated visual misinformation. Instruction should emphasize active verification strategies, particularly corroboration, and support students in coordinating multiple evaluation strategies when engaging with online visual information.
