### 1. Exploring collaborative problem-solving in AI-assisted robotics education
- 저자: Christian Basil Omeh, Elias Oupa Mashile  |  게재: 2026-10-16
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2749410](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2749410)

**초록:**
Prompt engineering (PE) involves creating, improving, and deploying prompts to improve large language model (LLM) performance. Despite its increasing importance, many students lack skills in this area. Although frameworks for prompt design and process exist, theory-based instructional tools for improving prompt engineering skills are uncommon for developing collaborative problem-solving (CPS) skills in Robotics Education. This study adopted a PE strategy in AI-assisted robotics education, particularly for developing CPS. PE has four scaffolding stages: Alignment, Adaptation, Autonomy, and Innovation, and was analysed through four PE codes: Alignment Specification, Adaptive Refinement, Auditing, and Innovation. This study adopted a non-randomised, multi-site, criterion-group comparative design, with 243 undergraduate students categorised according to their ability group. This study used epistemic network analysis (ENA) to identify the co-occurrences of PE and CPS skills and examine the differences in conversation patterns among ability groups. The findings revealed distinct PE–CPS discourse configurations across ability groups. These results underscore the pedagogical value of scaffolding in developing PE skills and fostering students’ productivity in a synergetic learning environment.

### 3. Comparing expert guided training and self-learning with virtual patients in emergency management training: a randomized controlled trial
- 저자: Dilek Kitapçıoğlu, Feray Güven, Emel Gur, Mehmet Emin Aksoy, Dilan Çabuk  |  게재: 2026-10-16
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2745728](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2745728)

**초록:**
Virtual patient-based simulation training has become a valuable tool in emergency medical education, particularly for developing clinical reasoning and decision-making skills. This randomized controlled experimental study compared the effectiveness of educator-guided and self-directed virtual patient training among paramedic students. Participants completed identical clinical scenarios using a virtual patient platform, either with real-time instructor support or independently with computer-generated feedback. Findings revealed that educator-guided training led to better performance across several domains of emergency assessment and management, especially in tasks requiring nuanced judgment and real-time decision-making. These results highlight the importance of instructional scaffolding and interpersonal feedback in simulation-based learning. While self-directed training offers flexibility and scalability, a blended approach that combines structured guidance with autonomous practice may provide the most comprehensive educational benefit for paramedic students. The study contributes to the growing evidence base for simulation pedagogy and supports the integration of instructor-led components into virtual patient training programs.

### 1. The Nexus of Innovation: Technological, Organizational, and Environmental Influences on VR Adoption in Libraries
- 저자: Yuping Shao, Abdullah Al Mamun, Mohammad Nurul Hassan Reza, Qing Yang  |  게재: 2026-10-16
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70355](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70355)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Virtual reality (VR) technology offers immersive, interactive, and engaging educational experiences. It can significantly improve how well students understand and retain their learning for complex subjects. Purpose This study explores the nexus of technological, organizational, and environmental factors influencing the readiness to adopt and the adoption of VR in academic libraries. Methods Drawing on the Technological‐Organizational‐Environmental model as the theoretical foundation, data were collected from 232 Chinese Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) using the stratified random sampling method and analysed using Partial Least Squares–Structural Equation Modelling. Results Factors from all three categories, including perceived benefits, monetary resources, information and communication technology infrastructure, HEIs support, and market dynamics, had significant positive effects on VR technology readiness towards its adoption, whereas perceived complexity had a negative effect. Moreover, VR technology readiness was a significant mediator in the relationships between the factors and VR technology adoption. Control variables, including HEIs ranking and number of students, and the Importance‐Performance Map yielded mixed results. Conclusions This study is novel as it extends the investigation of VR adoption, which has been predominantly explored in commercial and industrial contexts, to the underexplored setting of academic libraries, particularly within the Chinese context. Additionally, by integrating VR technology readiness as a mediating variable, this study offers unique insights into technology adoption in academic libraries, thereby addressing critical research gaps. The findings can aid HEIs administrators, policy makers, and educators in planning, assessing, and implementing VR‐based technologies, enabling them to gain a more comprehensive understanding of users' satisfaction and promote the sustained use of advanced VR systems.

### 2. Writing Motivation and Self‐Efficacy in AI ‐Chatbot‐Supported EFL Writing: Exploratory Insights Across Proficiency Levels
- 저자: Thi-Ngoc-Anh Duong, Hsiu‐Ling Chen  |  게재: 2026-10-15
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70356](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70356)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Writing motivation and self‐efficacy are critical factors of success in English as a Foreign Language (EFL) contexts, yet they are frequently undermined by learner anxiety and limited teacher feedback. While artificial intelligence (AI) chatbots offer potential for adaptive writing support, limited research has examined these affective constructs in parallel across learner proficiency levels. Objectives This study investigated changes in intrinsic motivation, extrinsic motivation, and writing self‐efficacy among high school EFL learners at higher‐ and lower‐proficiency levels following chatbot‐supported writing and examined whether these changes differed across proficiency levels. Methods Using an exploratory mixed‐methods design, the study involved 47 Vietnamese high school students divided into higher‐ and lower‐proficiency level groups. Over the 11‐week study, participants completed 8 weeks of AI‐supported writing activities using a chatbot designed in accordance with principles of Self‐Determination Theory, Self‐Regulated Learning Theory and Self‐Efficacy Theory. Changes in intrinsic motivation, extrinsic motivation, and writing self‐efficacy were examined using pre‐ and post‐questionnaires and semi‐structured interviews. Results and Conclusions The quantitative findings revealed that intrinsic motivation, extrinsic motivation, and writing self‐efficacy increased over time in both proficiency groups, with no significant differences in the magnitude of change between higher‐ and lower‐proficiency learners. This suggests that the affective changes observed during AI‐supported writing were not confined to one proficiency group. The qualitative findings indicated that learners linked their positive experiences to reduced anxiety, clearer guidance, and supportive interaction, although the types of support they valued differed by proficiency level. Overall, the findings suggest that when positioned as a supportive writing partner rather than an answer generator, AI chatbot feedback may be associated with positive motivational and self‐efficacy development in mixed‐ability EFL writing classrooms.

### 3. The Influence of the Role of Peer Presence on Game‐Based Chemistry Learning: Evidence From EEG
- 저자: Qian Huangfu, Yujia Guo, Tingting Deng, 黃志方  |  게재: 2026-10-16
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70361](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70361)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Game‐based learning (GBL) has become an important way to innovate chemistry teaching. However, it is still unclear how different peer roles in GBL shape learners' learning outcomes and cognitive processes. Objectives The study examined how different peer roles (individual vs. collaborators vs. competitors) influence learners' outcomes in game‐based chemistry learning. It also examined differences in theta and alpha power across these conditions. Methods The study employed a between‐subjects design with 69 ninth‐grade students from a secondary school in China who had not yet learned the target chemical concepts. Students were randomly assigned to individual, collaborative or competitive learning. Outcome measurement included immediate and delayed testing, motivation, cognitive load, interest and electroencephalography (EEG)‐based alpha and theta power. Results and Conclusions Both the collaborative and competitive conditions showed higher learning performance and interest than the individual condition. The collaborative condition also showed higher motivation than both the competitive and individual conditions and higher cognitive load than the individual condition. EEG analyses showed that the collaborative condition elicited higher theta and alpha power than the individual condition across both phases, although differences from the competitive condition were not consistently significant. These patterns may reflect greater demands on cognitive control and selective processing during collaborative learning. Implications The findings highlight the value of collaborative peer roles in game‐based chemistry learning. Teachers may consider incorporating structured collaborative activities to promote students' engagement and cognitive involvement. The findings also suggest that different peer roles may serve distinct motivational functions, which may inform the design of GBL activities.
