### 1. Research Trends on the <scp>TPACK</scp> in Science Education From 2009 to 2023: A Systematic Content Analysis
- 저자: İdris Aktaş, Haluk Özmen  |  게재: 2026-07-27
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70287](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70287)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background The number of TPACK studies has increased significantly in recent years, aiming to explore effective ways to support teachers. However, subject‐specific content has received limited attention, especially within review‐based research. Therefore, there is a need for a systematic analysis of TPACK studies in the context of subject content. Objectives This study aims to reveal the research trends of articles published on TPACK in science education from 2009 to 2023 through systematic content analysis. Methods A total of 90 articles published in English retrieved from the Web of Science, ERIC, and Scopus databases were analysed. The articles were analysed according to the following themes: research rationales, research objectives, examined variables, technological tools/teaching methods/science content domains, research methods, implementation processes, sampling groups, data collection tools, and main results. Results The articles were generally carried out to determine the TPACK levels of science teachers and to develop their competencies for integrating technology into science teaching such as wikis, Facebook, WhatsApp, Moodle, 3D technologies, mobile devices, virtual reality, or new teaching methods such as inquiry‐based instruction supported by technology. Many studies reported that teachers demonstrated relatively low or moderate levels of TPACK, including in some studies conducted after professional development programs. In recent years the articles focused on new research areas such as examining the impact of TPACK‐based science teaching on student learning outcomes, developing teachers' inquiry‐based teaching skills with digital technologies, and linking TPACK with educational robotics, ICT, and STEM education in science education. Conclusion TPACK‐based professional development programs in science education improve teachers' professional knowledge and skills. TPACK research has expanded with new pedagogical methods and technological advances. However, domain‐specific TPACK remains underexplored.

### 2. Human or <scp>GenAI</scp> Support? Conditions Impacting Students' Strategy Choices in an Essay Revision Task
- 저자: Sehrish Iqbal, Jelena Jovanović, Yizhou Fan, Mladen Raković, Xinyu Li et al.  |  게재: 2026-07-27
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70300](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70300)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Students often struggle to effectively self‐regulate their learning strategies during writing tasks and typically require external support from teachers, peers or AI tools to overcome these challenges. While emerging research explores how generative AI can dialogically interact with students to support the writing process, little is known about how such AI support influences writing strategies during the essay‐revision task. Furthermore, there is limited understanding of how GenAI support compares to other external conditions (human feedback or no support) in shaping writing strategies and how it interacts with internal learning factors such as writing skills, metacognitive accuracy and learning motivation. Objectives Our study aims to investigate the relationship of self‐regulated learning (SRL) strategies that students adopt for an essay‐revision task with learning conditions (both internal and external) and learning performance. Methods Using a technology‐enhanced learning environment, we collected trace data from 87 participants during essay‐writing and revision tasks. We applied Mixture Markov Models (MMMs) to model the writing/revision processes, then used the resulting transition matrix within the Expectation–Maximization (EM) clustering algorithm to identify SRL strategies. A series of statistical tests were applied to examine the relationship of strategies that students adopt for a revision task with learning conditions (internal and external) and learning performance. Results and Conclusions We found moderate associations between writing strategies during the essay‐revision task and internal conditions (writing strategies adopted in the preceding essay‐writing task) and large associations with external conditions (external support). However, no association was found between writing strategies during the essay‐revision task and learning performance. These findings inform the development of educational interventions to improve students' writing strategies.

### 3. Can Generative <scp>AI</scp> Empower Tibetan Students in English Writing? Exploring Performance Gains, Self‐Efficacy Enhancement and Cultural Expression
- 저자: Ming Cheng  |  게재: 2026-07-27
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70298](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70298)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Tibetan university students often write in English within a complex multilingual environment involving Tibetan, Chinese and English. Previous studies have suggested that multilingual learners may experience challenges related to cross‐linguistic transfer, as well as difficulties in expressing culturally specific concepts in English writing. At the same time, limited research has examined how contextually relevant AI‐supported feedback is experienced in minority‐language educational contexts. Objective This study explored associations between participation in a contextually relevant GenAI‐supported writing intervention (implemented through the Doubao app) and Tibetan college students' English writing performance, self‐efficacy (SE) and experiences of cultural expression in English writing. Methods A 16‐week quasi‐experiment was conducted with 60 Tibetan students paired into an experimental group (EG, n = 30, using AI with cultural support) and a control group (CG, n = 30, traditional teaching). Data were collected through writing tests, an SE scale and semi‐structured interviews. Results The EG demonstrated higher scores than the CG on several writing measures, particularly Vocabulary and Language Use, and these differences remained evident in the delayed post‐test. Positive patterns were also observed across writing SE measures. Interview data suggested that some participants perceived cultural terminology support and contrastive linguistic feedback as helpful during writing and revision activities. Conclusion The findings suggest that participation in a contextually relevant AI‐supported writing intervention was associated with positive writing outcomes among Tibetan trilingual learners. Participants perceived feedback addressing Tibetan–English structural differences and culturally specific terminology as helpful during writing tasks. Further research is needed to better understand the specific contribution of these context‐sensitive features.

### 4. From Competency to Agency: Teachers' Views of the Purposes of <scp>AI</scp> Education
- 저자: Janne Fagerlund, Pekka Mertala, Jukka Lehtoranta, Emilia Mattila, Laura Salo et al.  |  게재: 2026-07-27
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70302](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70302)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background As artificial intelligence (AI) becomes an increasingly prominent societal and educational phenomenon, teachers are required to determine not only how but also why students are educated about it in the classroom. Understanding teachers' thinking about the purposes of AI education is crucial, as they guide day‐to‐day choices about classroom uses of AI. Objectives This qualitative study investigates the purposes that teachers associate with AI education in primary and early childhood contexts and how AI has been taken up in teaching and learning. Methods Drawing on Gert Biesta's three‐dimensional framework of well‐rounded educational purpose, which emphasises students' holistic growth through the domains of qualification, socialisation, and subjectification, this study presents an abductive analysis of interview data from 13 Finnish teachers involved in national AI education development projects. Results and Conclusions The teachers' views reflected an exploratory phase of AI education, characterised by an emphasis on skill development (qualification); a focus that risks overlooking students' critical and personal engagement with these prominent technologies. It also triggers diverse interpretations and teaching practices, ranging from an instrumental approach, where education is guided by goals such as future employability, to an orientation that foregrounds emancipation and self‐expression in the present. While the teachers acknowledged the importance of fostering AI agency (subjectification), their approaches lacked robust strategies to support it. This study foregrounds such gaps and conceptualises a potential synergy between the domains where students' self‐determined, informed engagement with AI is placed at the centre of educational efforts in AI education.

### 5. Development and Validation of the <scp>AI</scp> Learning, Metacognitive, and Epistemic Barriers Scale ( <scp>AIME</scp> ‐B) for Secondary School Principals in Jordan
- 저자: Yusra Jadallah Abed Khasawneh, Najwa Ahmad Saleem Khasawneh, Mohamad Ahmad Saleem Khasawneh, Nabil Saleh Sufyan, Asma Jadallah Khasawneh  |  게재: 2026-07-27
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70301](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70301)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Rapid adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) in education is reshaping teaching, learning, and administration. Beyond technical skills, AI integration requires cognitive, metacognitive/self‐regulatory, and epistemic preparedness; thus, latent barriers may constrain how secondary school principals interpret, evaluate, and use AI‐generated information in decision‐making, creating a need for a psychometrically sound instrument. Objectives This study aimed (1) to develop and (2) to validate the AI Learning, Metacognitive, and Epistemic Barriers Scale (AIME‐B) for secondary school principals in Jordan, to assess cognitive, metacognitive/self‐regulatory, and epistemic constraints that hinder reflective, context‐appropriate engagement with AI outputs in school administration and decision‐making. Methods Using an exploratory mixed‐methods design, data were collected from 712 secondary school principals in Jordan (2025). The initial psychometric form contained 40 items; following item purification, the final scale retained 38 items loading on six factors. Results and Conclusions Internal consistency was high (α = 0.891–0.958; ω = 0.880–0.958), and within‐model convergent evidence was acceptable (AVE &gt; 0.50; CR &gt; 0.84). The first‐order CFA supported six correlated dimensions. Revision‐stage comparisons using the item‐level confirmatory dataset favoured the correlated six‐factor model over one‐factor, four‐factor, two theoretically plausible five‐factor, second‐order, and bifactor alternatives. The bifactor solution did not support a strong general factor, reinforcing primary interpretation of the six subscales. Gender invariance and bootstrapped EGA provided supplementary evidence regarding score comparability and clustering stability. Overall, the AIME‐B provides initial, context‐specific evidence supporting the internal structure and internal consistency of scores among Jordanian secondary school principals; temporal stability, external validity, and cross‐context generalizability require further study.

### 6. The Effects of Out‐of‐Class <scp>AI</scp> ‐Generated Feedback on <scp>EFL</scp> Learners' Writing Achievement and Emotions: Evidence From a Quasi‐Experimental Study
- 저자: Ge Qu, Zhenhua Lyu, Qing Yang  |  게재: 2026-07-27
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70293](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70293)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Research has investigated how AI‐generated feedback affects L2 writing development. Less attention has been paid, however, to students' emotional experiences when using generative AI tools in informal language learning or to how these experiences relate to writing outcomes. Objectives This study examined EFL learners' writing performance and emotional experiences in relation to their use of AI‐generated feedback beyond the classroom. Methods Guided by the broaden‐and‐build theory, this quasi‐experimental mixed‐methods study involved 110 Chinese EFL learners enrolled in an Academic Writing course in northern China. Participants were recruited from two intact Academic Writing classes, with one existing class assigned to the experimental group ( n = 55) and the other existing class assigned to the control group ( n = 55). Both groups received the same classroom instruction on academic writing. Compared with the control group, the experimental group received additional guidance on how to use ChatGPT to generate out‐of‐class writing feedback and engaged in self‐directed AI‐supported writing revision outside the classroom. The final writing assessment was conducted in class under comparable conditions for both groups. Writing performance was evaluated using an analytic rubric, and learners' anxiety, enjoyment and boredom were measured by established self‐report scales. Open‐ended responses were analysed inductively. Results and Conclusions The experimental group achieved significantly higher writing scores and reported significantly lower anxiety, boredom and enjoyment than the control group. Qualitative data showed that learners found AI‐generated feedback helpful for organising ideas and improving language use; however, they also expressed concerns about communication issues and over‐dependence on AI tools. These findings contribute to research on AI‐mediated informal digital learning of English and suggest that AI‐generated feedback may be associated with both L2 writing development and learners' emotional experiences.
