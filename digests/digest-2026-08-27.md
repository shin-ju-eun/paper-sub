## International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education (2편)

### 1. What students ask matters: LLM interaction depth, task quality, and immediate recall in higher education
- 저자: Vangelis Tsiligkiris  |  게재: 2026-08-25
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00617-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00617-6)

**초록:**
Abstract Large Language Models (LLMs) are rapidly transforming higher education, yet evidence on how interaction patterns affect learning remains limited. This study examines whether explanation-seeking dialogue with an LLM is associated with task quality and immediate recall in a controlled learning session. Twenty-two postgraduate students completed a pre-test, an LLM-assisted neuroeconomics case task, and an immediate post-test. Fine-grained interaction logs captured per-turn telemetry, enabling extraction of Depth (proportion of "why/how/explain" prompts), Volume, and Pacing features. Results showed large immediate pre–post score gains (Cohen’s d z = 2.12, p < .001). In the task-quality regression model, Depth was positively associated with task quality beyond baseline and Volume (β = 6.27, p = .006), indicating that a one-standard-deviation increase in explanation-seeking prompts was associated with approximately six additional marks on a 0–100 scale. Depth was not associated with immediate recall (β = − 0.014, p = .728); instead, gain scores were strongly associated with baseline knowledge, consistent with reduced headroom for improvement among students starting from a higher pre-test score (β = − 0.161, p < .001). The findings indicate a dissociation between performance quality and short-term recall in LLM-supported study. This aligns with cognitive-psychology evidence that elaboration improves comprehension while retrieval practice consolidates retention. Pedagogically, the dissociation suggests that depth-oriented dialogue may need to be paired with deliberate memory-strengthening activities such as self-testing or spaced retrieval if comprehension gains are to translate into recall, although the present design does not test such activities directly. Methodologically, it contributes a replicable, privacy-preserving instrumentation pipeline linking conversational telemetry to learning outcomes. Limitations include single-group design, small sample ( n = 22), immediate testing only, and keyword-based depth proxies. Future work should randomise assistance styles, incorporate delayed retention tests, and refine depth measurement through semantic coding.

### 2. From learning analytics to educational interventions: enhancing decision-making and learning design
- 저자: Barbi Svetec, Blaženka Divjak, Nikola Kadoić  |  게재: 2026-08-26
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00620-x](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00620-x)

**초록:**
Abstract Since the early development of learning analytics (LA), the cyclical nature of the process that starts with learning data and ends with educational interventions has been widely acknowledged. However, while LA research abounds, and LA practices are becoming more widespread, the final step of the cycle – introducing educational interventions - remains a challenge. The recently revised definition of LA puts a stronger focus on actionable insights to enhance learning and teaching. Recognizing that only trustworthy LA presents a basis for meaningful educational interventions, we conducted a study relying on group decision-making to explore and determine the key enablers of LA-based educational interventions. Our findings revealed seven enablers: institutional strategic orientation , pedagogical & other research foundations , available resources , pedagogical support , ethics & data governance , stakeholder engagement , and quality assurance . These enablers provide a foundation for the development and use of trustworthy LA systems to support data-informed decision-making, learning design, and educational change.

### 1. Analysing the role of technology-enabled learning systems in women’s empowerment toward higher education
- 저자: Khalida Parveen, Tribhuwan Kumar, Abdulelah A. Alghamdi, Muhammad Shafiq  |  게재: 2026-08-25
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2696364](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2696364)

**초록:**
This research explores the effect of Technology-Enabled Learning Systems (TELS) on women’s empowerment in higher education in Pakistan. The primary goal is to analyse factors influencing women’s engagement with technology-enabled learning systems, including online learning platforms, mobile learning applications, educational apps, and virtual classroom technologies, including behavioural intention, social influence, perceived usefulness, personal requirements, and technical/electronic requirements. Through a literature review and quantitative analysis, the study aims to uncover the intricate interplay of these factors in fostering women’s empowerment. Using convenience sampling, the experiences of female faculty members in Pakistani universities are examined, providing nuanced insights. The investigation relies on a hypothesis as the foundational element for analysis, utilising the Structural Equation Model through the softwares SPSS and AMOS to assess and confirm the connections among variables. The study’s findings demonstrate that behavioural intention, social influence, perceived usefulness, and technical and electronic requirements significantly impact women’s empowerment within the framework of higher education in Pakistan. Additionally, practical recommendations will be provided for stakeholders seeking to improve women’s contribution and success in higher education through technology-enabled learning systems.

### 2. The selective enhancement hypothesis: how immersive VR differentially affects learning dimensions in heritage literature education
- 저자: Ruijuan Yin, Yi Zhang, Sha Xu, Yangyang Gui  |  게재: 2026-08-26
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2721384](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2721384)

**초록:**
Heritage literature education faces a persistent challenge: texts central to cultural identity are often difficult to engage with, particularly at the level of interpretive understanding. Although immersive virtual reality (VR) shows promise in education, existing research largely treats learning as a unitary outcome, leaving its effects across knowledge dimensions unclear. We propose the Selective Enhancement Hypothesis (SEH), which predicts that VR benefits are dimension-specific rather than uniform. Across four knowledge dimensions, effects should be largest for interpretive understanding, smaller for literal comprehension and cultural knowledge, and minimal for contextual knowledge. Drawing on embodied cognition, construal level, and cognitive load theories, we account for this gradient through two ordering dimensions: sensorimotor dependence and constructive complexity. We identify cultural-world vividness, defined as the perceptual richness and spatial coherence of learners’ mental representations of a text’s cultural world, as the primary mediating mechanism. The framework addresses three persistent gaps in VR education research: the aggregation of learning outcomes, the absence of active controls, and limited attention to retention, through a three-group experimental design incorporating behavioral, self-report, and retention measures. SEH thus shifts VR education research from aggregate efficacy claims toward testable, dimension-specific predictions about where embodied immersion uniquely supports learning.

### 1. Collaborative Learning: A Transactional Approach to Examining Collaborative Processes and Outcomes
- 저자: Hanni Muukkonen, Jun Oshima, Crina Damşa  |  게재: 2026-08-25
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70306](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70306)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Research on collaborative learning has long examined processes and outcomes separately, with distinct fields—Computer‐Supported Collaborative Learning (CSCL), learning sciences and recently learning analytics—drawing on differing epistemological premises. Integrated analytical accounts connecting collaboration processes to the outcomes of collaboration remain scarce. This special issue addresses that gap by studies of collaborative learning that employ conceptualisations and analytical methods aligned with a transactional approach. Contributions in This Special Issue The issue comprises an editorial, seven empirical studies and two commentaries. Collectively, the studies show that scaffolding collaboration can target reflection, regulation, motivation, knowledge‐object development and learner–technology relations, each mediating transactions differently. Using multimodal and multilayered analytical approaches—including epistemic network analysis, multi‐layered temporal network analysis, sequence mining and multilevel modelling—the contributions examine how tools, artefacts, discourse and motivational states jointly shape learning outcomes across authentic educational settings, from higher education to school and after‐school contexts. The commentaries situate these findings within broader theoretical and methodological debates in CSCL and research that emphasises understanding the relational and situated nature of collaborative learning. Implications for Future Research The authenticity of the studied settings carries implications for data collection, analysis and design for collaborative learning, offering insight for researchers, teachers, learning designers and technology developers. Future research should give attention to the relational, temporal, multimodal aspects combined—that is, employ an integrative approach in the interpretation and revisit conceptualisations that integrate material‐ecological, epistemic‐discursive and motivational‐psychological perspectives on the relationship between collaboration processes and outcomes.

### 2. Deceptive Overgeneralization: When Adaptive Learning Enables Systematic Misapplication
- 저자: Marshall An, Bruce M. McLaren, John Stamper  |  게재: 2026-08-25
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70311](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70311)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Adaptive learning systems infer mastery from observed correctness, but this approach may fall short when learners exhibit ‘deceptive overgeneralization’—performing correct actions based on incomplete understanding. This phenomenon can mislead systems into prematurely stopping practice, leaving knowledge gaps unaddressed. Clarifying when deceptive overgeneralization occurs and how to address it can improve the precision of adaptive learning technologies. Objectives This research empirically examines whether deceptive overgeneralization occurs in adaptive learning, assesses its consequences for mastery‐based stopping rules, and evaluates a systematic procedure for detecting and remedying it. Methods We conducted 11 experiments using Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITSs) for Riichi Mahjong across multiple semesters with university students (total N = 192). The studies employed a pretest–posttest design to assess ITS effectiveness, and within‐subjects designs with adaptive and full‐exposure conditions to examine overgeneralization and remediation. Tailored remediation consisted of targeted ‘do‐not‐act’ problems with feedback that names the missing application constraints. Results and Conclusions ITSs improved learning outcomes, but learners frequently misapplied acquired actions in contexts where they should have refrained, indicating deceptive overgeneralization. Under adaptive learning, mastery stopping rules often ended practice before learners encountered such ‘do‐not‐act’ contexts, leaving the issue undetected. Tailored remediation using targeted ‘do‐not‐act’ problems with constraint‐naming feedback reduced misapplication to near‐floor levels across skills and cohorts, highlighting a limitation of correctness‐based mastery inference and a practical method to strengthen mastery assessment for knowledge compilation. A secondary analysis of K–12 decimal learning data further illustrates how the framework applies in a traditional educational domain.
