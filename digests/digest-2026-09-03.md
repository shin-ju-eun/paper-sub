## Interactive Learning Environments (2편)

### 1. What drives students to learn with GenAI? A dual-pathway model of cognitive and socio-affective mechanisms
- 저자: Juan Chen, Fang Deng  |  게재: 2026-09-01
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2728026](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2728026)

**초록:**
This study proposes and empirically tests a dual-pathway integrative model to explain the psychological mechanisms underlying Chinese undergraduate students’ adoption and use of GenAI for learning. Combining cognitive appraisal theory, task-technology fit and socio-affective factors, the model examines how cognitive-utility and socio-affective pathways shape students’ appraisals, emotions, and behavioral intentions. Data from 794 students were analyzed using PLS-SEM. Results show that the cognitive-utility pathway, particularly task-technology fit, strongly predicts challenge appraisal and behavioral intention, while socio-affective factors exert more nuanced effects. Challenge and hindrance appraisals directly influence intention, with positive emotion mediating the former. Gender moderated only one socio-affective path, while academic discipline showed no significant moderation. The study contributes to theoretical integration in technology adoption and provides guidance for educators on effectively integrating GenAI in higher education.

## Journal of Computer Assisted Learning (3편)

### 1. Teacher Readiness‐By‐Design for AI ‐Rich Interactive Learning
- 저자: Tendai Charles  |  게재: 2026-09-01
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70316](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70316)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Artificial intelligence (AI)‐rich educational technologies are expanding rapidly, yet teacher readiness is still commonly represented through adoption, confidence or competence constructs that do not explain the quality of the learning designs produced. Objective This conceptual article develops readiness‐by‐design as an enactment‐oriented capability: teachers' capacity to mobilise professional resources through design mediation, within an enabling institutional and policy ecology, to create pedagogically aligned, inclusive and ethically defensible AI‐mediated learning. Approach An iterative theory‐building synthesis integrates research on technology acceptance, teacher digital competence, AI literacy, TPACK, teacher design capacity and agency, responsible AI, institutional support and instructional design. The literature‐identification and construct‐integration logic is made explicit, and recent empirical studies from 2024 to 2026 are used to interrogate the framework. Contribution and Conclusions The framework is distinctive because it specifies design mediation as the mechanism linking teacher resources to observable instructional design quality, positions policy clarity and institutional support as moderating conditions, incorporates feedback from enacted designs and treats K‐12 and higher education as boundary conditions. Six directional propositions, candidate indicators and a design‐based professional‐learning model provide a basis for empirical validation.

### 2. Enhancing Peer Exposure and Creating Opportunities for Bridging Social Capital Through an AI ‐Generated Summary‐Driven Learning Design in Online Discussion Forums
- 저자: Xinyuan Hao, Mutlu Cukurova  |  게재: 2026-09-01
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70317](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70317)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Providing discussion summaries and examples has long been recognised as an effective strategy to enhance students' engagement in online learning environments. While prior research has primarily focused on human‐written summaries and their influence on observable learning behaviours, recent advances in large language models and artificial intelligence (AI) have enabled both the generation of high‐quality AI‐produced discussion summaries and example posts and increased scholarly attention to this AI‐generated support. However, existing research has largely examined the technical performance, and little is known about the educational impact of such AI‐generated learning support, particularly in relation to students' peer interactions and the development of social capital in online forums. Objectives This study aimed to examine the impacts of an AI‐generated summary‐driven learning design (AI‐SLD) on students' viewing engagement and bridging social capital in online discussion forums. Methods A design‐based research approach was conducted with 128 university students over an eight‐week intervention. Forum log data was analysed using social network analysis to investigate changes in students' viewing activities and opportunities for bridging social capital. Thematic analysis of student interviews was undertaken to explain the behavioural and relational patterns. Results and Conclusions The findings indicate that AI‐SLD broadened students' exposure to peer contributions, strengthened overall network connectedness, and increased the opportunities for developing bridging social capital. AI‐generated summaries functioned as navigational scaffolds, helping students locate diverse perspectives more efficiently. However, AI‐SLD did not prevent the decrease in viewing activities in later weeks, suggesting that external academic workload moderated engagement.

### 3. The Effect of Virtual Reality in Nasogastric Catheter Application Training for Nursing Students: A Randomized Controlled Trial
- 저자: Ayşe Eminoğlu, Nazike Duruk, Ibrahim Savran  |  게재: 2026-09-01
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70314](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70314)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background With advances in technology, innovative teaching methods are being used to teach nasogastric catheterization. Aim This study was conducted to evaluate the effects of training in nasogastric catheterization, delivered via traditional and virtual reality methods, on the knowledge, skills, self‐confidence, anxiety and motivation of final‐year nursing students. Methods This randomized controlled experimental design study was conducted with 78 fourth‐year nursing students (39 in the intervention group and 39 in the control group) between March and May 2024. Written permission was obtained from the ethics committee, the institution and the students. Data were collected via the ‘Personal Information Form’, ‘Nasogastric Catheterization Application Knowledge Test’, ‘Nasogastric Catheter Application Checklist’, ‘Self‐Confidence Visual Analog Scale’, ‘Anxiety Visual Analog Scale’ and ‘Instructional Material Motivation Scale for Single‐use’. All the students were trained according to the course content. After the training, the students in the intervention group used the virtual‐reality application, and the students in the control group used it with the traditional method. After the application, the students' skills were evaluated and post‐tests were applied. The data were evaluated using chi‐square analysis, independent‐samples t ‐tests and two‐way analysis of variance with repeated measures. Results There was no significant difference in knowledge and motivation scores between the intervention and control groups ( p > 0.05). The students in the intervention group had significantly greater nasogastric catheterization skills, critical procedure step scores and self‐confidence scores and lower anxiety scores than did those in the control group ( p < 0.05). Conclusion Virtual reality was more effective than traditional methods at improving students' nasogastric catheterization skills, increasing their self‐confidence and reducing their anxiety. Therefore, the use of virtual reality to teach psychomotor skills, such as nasogastric catheterization, should be expanded. Trial Registration ClinicalTrials.gov registration number was received as NCT05815979
