## International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education (1편)

### 1. Integrating LLM with consortium blockchain for personalized and verifiable online education in higher education
- 저자: Fuan Xiao, Jiahui Huang, Jia-Xin Huang, Huali Ren, Lang Li  |  게재: 2026-08-11
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00618-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00618-5)

**초록:**
Abstract Online Education in Higher Education is rapidly evolving through the integration of Large Language Model (LLM)-powered intelligent systems, which enable personalized tutoring, dynamic content generation, and automated assessment. However, the widespread adoption of LLMs in education is hampered due to their inherent limitations, including susceptibility to hallucinations, insufficient domain-specific knowledge validation, and output inconsistency. These deficiencies can lead to misleading or erroneous content, potentially causing significant negative learning outcomes. A core challenge lies in ensuring that such errors are immutably logged and traceable, thereby establishing a mechanism for accountability among the entities deploying these LLM services. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a novel framework that Integrates LLM with consortium blockchain for personalized and verifiable online education. Our design features a synergistic architecture in which LLM based services provide the intelligent educational interface, while a permissioned consortium blockchain serves as a secure and tamper proof ledger. This blockchain infrastructure records critical educational transactions ranging from learning process data and academic credentials to the outputs generated by the LLMs. This integration not only secures academic credentials but also establishes a fully auditable trail, making it possible to trace responsibility for educational deficiencies caused by AI errors. Collectively, this work demonstrates a robust and accountable framework for leveraging LLMs in education, effectively mitigating the risks of AI inaccuracies through the verifiable and immutable nature of consortium blockchain.

## Interactive Learning Environments (3편)

### 1. How do AI feedback types affect students’ continuance usage intention in AI hallucinations?
- 저자: Yupeng Guo, Tinglan Huang, Hao Lu, Li Zong, Lehui Huang et al.  |  게재: 2026-08-10
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2711950](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2711950)

**초록:**
AI hallucinations threaten the sustainable use of generative AI in higher education, yet prior research has largely emphasized technical detection rather than students’ psychological responses and socio-emotional repair. This study examined how faithfulness and factuality hallucinations affect university students’ trust and tolerance, and how empathy- and humor-based repair strategies shape trust, tolerance, and continuance usage intention. Using a 2 × 2 × 2 mixed design, 400 university students participated in two educational scenarios. Mixed-effects models and bootstrap mediation analyses showed that factuality hallucinations elicited lower trust and tolerance than faithfulness hallucinations. Across both scenarios, high empathy combined with self-deprecating humor produced the most favorable outcomes. Trust and tolerance independently mediated the relationship between repair strategies and continuance usage intention, whereas serial mediation was not supported. These findings highlight the value of socio-emotional repair in designing educational AI systems that maintain student engagement after hallucination-related failures.

### 3. Metaverse-based blood transfusion education in midwifery: a mixed-methods study of knowledge outcomes and learner experiences
- 저자: Halime Aydemir, Enver Türksoy, Fatma Ergün  |  게재: 2026-08-11
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2698255](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2698255)

**초록:**
Metaverse-based learning is increasingly discussed in health professions education. This mixed-methods study examined whether Metaverse-based, online, and face-to-face instruction differed in their effects on safe blood transfusion knowledge and how students in the Metaverse group perceived their learning experience. Ninety-seven first-year midwifery students at Kirsehir Ahi Evran University, Turkey, participated in a quasi-experimental pretest-posttest design with three instructional conditions: Metaverse (n = 33), online (n = 32), and face-to-face (n = 32). All groups received the same content through four 90-min sessions delivered by the same instructor. Knowledge was assessed using the Safe Blood Transfusion Achievement Test. The test showed acceptable internal consistency in the pilot study (KR-21 = .71) and in the present dataset (Cronbach’s alpha = .742; McDonald’s omega = .773). ANCOVA indicated that, after controlling for pretest scores, adjusted post-test scores did not differ significantly across groups, F(2, 93) = 2.60, p = .079, partial eta squared = .053. Qualitative data were collected from 33 Metaverse-group students through structured Know-Want to Know-Learned reflections and analysed thematically. Students described the Metaverse as enjoyable, less socially threatening, and supportive of perceived freedom of movement and avatar-mediated participation. These results may offer a learner experience characterised by perceived emotional comfort, engagement, and agency.

## Journal of Research on Technology in Education (1편)

### 1. Beyond the tools: How different forms of cognitive engagement with technology relate to multidimensional student engagement
- 저자: Junxin Zhao, Geert Vandermeersche, Jerich Faddar, Koen Lombaerts  |  게재: 2026-08-10
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2026.2711658](https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2026.2711658)

**초록:**
Digital technologies are widely used in education, yet findings on their relationship with student engagement are inconsistent, limiting actionable insights. This is partly due to the neglect of cognitive processes involved in technology use. This study fills the gap by using the cognitive engagement with technology framework to investigate how retrieving, processing, and generating influence students’ behavioral, emotional, and cognitive engagement. Structural equation modeling revealed that processing positively predicted all engagement dimensions, retrieving predicted emotional engagement, and generating negatively predicted behavioral engagement. In addition, behavioral engagement mediated the link between emotional and cognitive engagement. These findings highlight how specific cognitive interactions with technology drive engagement, offering evidence-based guidance for designing technology-enhanced learning environments that move beyond mere tool access to purposeful cognitive activity.
