## Interactive Learning Environments (2편)

### 1. Critical yet dependent? Time poverty, critical AI engagement, and generative AI dependence in university interactive learning environments
- 저자: Xiang Che, Qi Hui  |  게재: 2026-11-06
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2754913](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2754913)

**초록:**
Generative AI has become an interactive learning resource, yet repeated delegation to AI may also coincide with difficulty working without it. This cross-sectional questionnaire study examined theory-consistent covariance patterns among time poverty, AI self-efficacy, perceived social support, AI critical thinking, effort enjoyment belief (EEB), and generative AI dependence in 1,862 first-year students from three higher-education institutions in Northwest China. Time poverty and AI critical thinking were positively associated with dependence, while AI self-efficacy and PSS were positively associated with AI critical thinking. In the primary specification, EEB moderated the time poverty-AI critical thinking and AI critical thinking-dependence associations, although both interactions were small. The findings distinguish critical engagement with AI from reported reliance and highlight time pressure as a potentially important feature of students’ AI use.

### 2. Beyond access: guided versus free ChatGPT use and learning outcomes in higher education
- 저자: Thseen Nazır  |  게재: 2026-11-06
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2759148](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2759148)

**초록:**
Research on artificial intelligence in higher education has largely examined usage patterns rather than the cognitive dimensions of student-AI interaction. This mixed-methods experimental study investigated how undergraduates’ epistemic engagement with ChatGPT during academic reasoning tasks relates to learning outcomes. Grounded in constructivist learning theory, the study randomly assigned 120 undergraduates to three conditions: No-AI control, Free-AI (unstructured access), and Guided-AI (process-focused scaffolding). Students completed epistemic reasoning tasks requiring conceptual understanding and judgment under ambiguity. Analysis identified four interaction profiles: Delegative (AI-led), Instrumental (procedural), Co-Constructive (shared control), and Dialogic-Critical (student-led critique). Task quality varied by interaction pattern rather than AI access alone; Delegative users showed no measurable advantage over No-AI controls despite having AI access. A minimal intervention, a single-page handout emphasizing questioning strategies, substantially increased dialogic engagement (d = 2.29) and critical evaluation (d = 2.19). Because profiles were observed rather than assigned, profile-outcome relationships are reported as associations. Findings challenge binary debates over AI prohibition versus permission, indicating that educational value is associated with students’ epistemic stance during interaction. Pedagogical scaffolding, rather than surveillance or restriction, may be the most productive institutional response to generative AI.

## Journal of Computer Assisted Learning (1편)

### 1. Student Responses to Cognitive Conflicts in GenAI ‐Assisted Learning: A Covariate‐Dependent Markov Chain Analysis on Human‐ AI Interactions
- 저자: Yiming Taclis Luo, Ting Liu, Patrick Cheong‐Iao Pang, Xiao Hu  |  게재: 2026-11-06
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70366](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70366)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background As generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) reshapes the learning process, students will inevitably experience cognitive conflicts when their existing knowledge and beliefs clash with information provided by GenAI. Improper handling of cognitive conflicts may lead to a decline in students' learning efficiency and even hinder the development of critical thinking skills. Objective This study aims to understand how students respond to cognitive conflicts during their interactions with GenAI, with a focus on comparing high‐performing and low‐performing student groups. Methods To identify relationships between cognitive conflict intensity and students' responses, we used a covariate‐dependent Markov chain (a sequential data modelling method) to analyse the response states of 411 students in a simulated academic writing task and observed transitions in student response states across different task performances. Results The results revealed that the intensity of cognitive conflict had a significant impact on students' state transitions. Overall, as the level of conflict increased, students' state changes exhibited a phased pattern, shifting from fluctuation (transitioning to a variety of different states) to inertia or convergence (maintaining the original state or transitioning uniformly to a negative response state). Student response state transitions varied across different task performance levels. Conclusions This study provides a novel methodological perspective for modelling sequential response dynamics in Human‐AI interaction. The findings offer new insights into the cognitive conflict dynamics in GenAI‐assisted learning, providing implications for designing GenAI‐assisted learning environments.
