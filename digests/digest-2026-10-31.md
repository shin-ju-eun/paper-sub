### 1. From Design to Classroom Adoption: Teachers' Perspectives on AI‐powered Writing Analytics in Resource‐Constrained Contexts
- 저자: Fabíola Gonçalves Coelho Ribeiro, Luiz Rodrigues, Cleon Xavier, Newarney Torrezão da Costa, Anderson Pinheiro Cavalcanti et al.  |  게재: 2026-10-30
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70362](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70362)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background The adoption of automated tools to support teaching and learning processes has been widely discussed in the literature. However, there remains a need for user‐oriented designs that better align with teachers' practices and classroom realities while providing actionable insights to support instructional choices in authentic educational settings. Objectives This study aims to systematically evaluate the usability, acceptability and pedagogical relevance of an AIED‐Unplugged learning analytics tool from teachers' perspectives, in order to guide the development of user‐centered design strategies and support writing practices in resource‐constrained educational settings without deepening digital inequities. The proposed study also aims to derive pedagogical and user‐centered design recommendations to strengthen the development of AIED‐U solutions for resource‐constrained educational settings. Method We employed a mixed‐methods approach involving nine teachers, who downloaded an AIED‐U application and followed a predefined usage scenario according to a think‐aloud protocol. Afterwards, participants filled out the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and System Usability Scale (SUS) questionnaires, taking part in a moderated semi‐structured interview guided by an interview script. Results and Conclusion The study shows how AI‐driven educational tools can support personalization and the development of essay‐writing activities in resource‐constrained contexts while optimizing teachers'time and contributing to students' writing development. The quantitative findings indicated high levels of acceptance and usability, corroborating the qualitative findings that teachers perceived the solution as useful, easy to integrate and supportive of their practices. Taken together, these findings provide empirical evidence of teachers' perceptions regarding the tool's usability, usefulness and potential applicability within authentic classroom workflows in resource‐constrained educational environments.

### 2. ChatGPT ‐Supported Literary Translation: Tense Accuracy and Dynamic Emotion Profiles in the Language Classroom
- 저자: Mariusz Kruk, Agnieszka Kałużna, Xian Zhao  |  게재: 2026-10-30
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70368](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70368)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Generative artificial intelligence can facilitate task completion, but whether it improves learning or consistently shapes task‐related emotions remains unclear. Objectives This mixed‐methods classroom study compared literary translation with ChatGPT access, translation with dictionary access and optional teacher assistance and regular instruction, focusing on tense accuracy and task‐related emotional experiences. Methods The study involved 42 English‐major undergraduates. All participants completed a pretest and a posttest of English tense use. The 29 students in the translation conditions completed six translation tasks and provided 556 repeated emotion‐bar records with concurrent written explanations; 10 of these students also participated in interviews. Robust analysis of covariance, within‐participant models, exploratory configurational measures and thematic analysis were used. Results Mean scores increased in all three conditions, but adjusted posttest scores and gain scores did not differ significantly among them. No statistically significant between‐condition differences were detected in individual‐emotion percentages or configurational measures. None of the six emotion percentages varied significantly across task sessions, although curiosity was lower at the fourth than at the first measurement point. Profile mixedness was lower in Task 5 (Session 6) than in Task 2 (Session 3). Qualitative findings linked emotions mainly to text content and difficulty, progress, time pressure and classroom conditions. ChatGPT was valued for its speed and immediacy but raised concerns about reliability, effort and ownership. Conclusion The study did not detect a statistically significant advantage for ChatGPT support in tense accuracy or emotional experience. The exploratory configurational analysis nevertheless identified a task‐session difference not apparent in the separate emotion percentages, illustrating the value of examining learning outcomes, repeated emotion reports and students' appraisals together when evaluating AI‐supported language tasks.

### 3. Beyond Access: How GenAI Literacy Relates to Task Performance, Motivation, Cognitive Load and Interaction Patterns in AI ‐Assisted Learning Environment
- 저자: Angxuan Chen, Huaiya Liu, Chunlei Ren, Jingjing LIAN, Jiyou Jia  |  게재: 2026-10-30
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70367](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70367)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Generative AI is rapidly being integrated into educational settings, yet equitable benefits depend heavily on students' GenAI literacy. While access to these tools is increasing, empirical evidence remains limited on how specific dimensions of assessed GenAI literacy relate to students' task performance, cognitive experiences, and actual interactional behaviours during learning. Objectives This study examined the associations of overall GenAI literacy and its four dimensions (Know and Understand, Use and Apply, Evaluate and Create, Ethics) with students' abstract‐writing performance, self‐efficacy, motivation, and cognitive load. It also explored how assessed literacy is reflected in the configurations of students' interactions with GenAI. Methods A total of 162 university students completed an abstract‐writing task using a custom GenAI assistant. Ordinary least‐squares regression models evaluated the continuous GenAI literacy scores against writing performance, motivation, and cognitive load, adjusting for prior academic writing knowledge and English proficiency. Additionally, Epistemic Network Analysis (ENA) was applied to conversational trace data to compare the configurations of questioning and commanding behaviours between higher‐ and lower‐literacy students. Results and Conclusions Higher overall GenAI literacy was positively associated with better writing performance and negatively associated with perceived mental load. Dimension‐level analyses revealed distinct roles: the Use and Apply dimension was independently associated with higher writing performance, while the Know and Understand dimension was uniquely associated with lower mental load. Neither the total score nor the sub‐dimensions showed reliable associations with motivation or self‐efficacy. Furthermore, ENA profiles demonstrated that higher‐literacy students engaged in a refinement‐oriented process, whereas lower‐literacy students relied predominantly on direct content generation and factual retrieval. These findings suggest that instruction should address both conceptual understanding and practical application of GenAI, helping students critically evaluate and refine AI‐generated content during writing.
