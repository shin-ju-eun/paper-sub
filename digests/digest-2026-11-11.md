## Interactive Learning Environments (2편)

### 1. A cognitive-pedagogical taxonomy of 3D modelling and printing activities in mathematics education: implications for digital creation
- 저자: Mathías Tejera, Thomas K. F. Chiu, Viviana Aharonian, Selen Galiç, Guillermo Bautista et al.  |  게재: 2026-11-10
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2754928](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2754928)

**초록:**
Digital creation activities can support learning through making, but their pedagogical value depends on how tasks organise cognitive engagement, learner autonomy, and participant roles. This study develops an empirically grounded taxonomy of three-dimensional modelling and printing (3DMP) activities in mathematics education. Drawing on the Interactive, Constructive, Active, and Passive (ICAP) framework, learner autonomy, and the distribution of teacher and learner roles, we analysed 119 activities from 38 learning designs using cluster analysis. The analysis identified four pedagogical profiles: Passive Demonstrations, Guided Explorations, Learner Modelling and Making, and Conceptual Co-Design. The profiles differed in their dominant ICAP modes, levels of learner autonomy, design roles, and pedagogical objectives, showing that autonomy, artefact production, collaboration, and cognitive engagement are related but non-equivalent aspects of 3DMP activity design. The taxonomy contextualises ICAP alongside autonomy and role distribution within 3DMP mathematics education, providing a multidimensional lens for comparing creation activities and a practical heuristic for activity design. Although these dimensions may also be relevant to other digital-creation technologies, the transferability of the four-profile structure requires independent empirical validation.

## Journal of Computer Assisted Learning (2편)

### 1. Examining the Effects of Motivating Language and Personalization in AI Voice‐Over Educational Videos: From a Social‐Psychological Perspective
- 저자: Yamei Zhang, Xiaojun Sun, Qingqing Ye  |  게재: 2026-11-10
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70375](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70375)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background The development of AI technology is transforming the production of videos. In particular, voice‐over video lectures can be easily produced with AI voice, which has been found to generate learning comparable to that of human voice. Further, verbal cues, which are social cues—critical factors impacting video‐based learning—expressed with words, can be readily implemented with AI voice. However, the influence of verbal cues in video lectures has been relatively underinvestigated, limiting the optimization of (AI) voice‐over educational videos. Objectives This study aims to explore the effects of motivating language and personalization, both of which are verbal cues—a common but underexplored type of social cues, on learning satisfaction, actual learning outcomes, perceived learning, and rapport in AI voice‐over video lectures. Methods A three (motivating language: autonomy‐supportive vs. controlling vs. neutral) × two (personalization: personalized vs. nonpersonalized) between‐subjects experiment was carried out among 182 university students. Results and Conclusions Motivating language impacted learning satisfaction, retention, perceived learning, and rapport, and the impacts on learning satisfaction and rapport varied with personalization; but collectively, autonomy‐supportive language was more effective than neutral language, whereas controlling language was ineffective, irrespective of personalization; besides, personalization showed benefits only under autonomy‐supportive language in rapport, and instead caused decreased learning satisfaction and rapport under neutral language. The findings have several theoretical implications, including deepening knowledge about verbal cues, extending theories about effects of social cues in multimedia learning, and broadening understanding of motivating teaching. Practical implications for the design of AI voice‐over video lectures were discussed.

### 2. Social Participatory Role Enactment in Asynchronous Discussion and Mobile Instant Messaging
- 저자: Yanyan Sun, 苏加叶, Chengjun Feng  |  게재: 2026-11-09
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70376](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70376)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Computer‐supported collaborative learning (CSCL) is widely used in higher education, yet participation remains uneven and discourse often stays at a low cognitive level. Asynchronous online discussion (AOD) and mobile instant messaging (MIM) are two common communication environments, but how social participatory roles are expressed through discourse across these environments remains unclear. Objectives This study examined conversational‐function and cognitive‐engagement profiles associated with social participatory roles in recorded AOD and MIM interactions. Methods Thirty first‐year undergraduates in eight groups completed two collaborative tasks using Blackboard forums (AOD) and Lark (MIM) in a counterbalanced cross‐over design. Social participatory roles were inferred via social network analysis; conversational functions were coded with the ASIMeC‐F scheme; and cognitive engagement was coded at four levels (operation, way‐finding, sense‐making, innovation). Data were analysed using paired role‐distribution comparisons, binary mixed‐effects logistic regression, descriptive role‐specific summaries, and epistemic network analysis (ENA). Results and Conclusions No difference in marginal role distributions was detected, although individual role transitions were common. Adjusted models associated AOD with a higher prevalence of Analysing and MIM with a higher prevalence of Respond and Commenting without elaboration. Role‐specific profiles illustrated different discourse patterns under similar role labels, while ENA described co‐occurrence patterns within the conversational‐function and cognitive‐engagement coding schemes. These findings inform a context‐sensitive account of role enactment and suggest role‐based scaffolding for further testing.
