## International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education (1편)

### 1. Learning with GenAI across campuses: student experiences of GenAI-enhanced cybersecurity education
- 저자: Sourabh Aggarwal  |  게재: 2026-11-05
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00639-0](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00639-0)

**초록:**
Abstract Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) is increasingly embedded in higher education, yet limited research has examined how GenAI-supported learning operates alongside geographically distributed peer collaboration. This qualitative study explores student experiences in an AI-enhanced cross-campus cybersecurity learning environment connecting university campuses in California, United States, and Ontario, Canada. Anonymous reflections were collected from 19 students (nine in California and ten in Ontario) about their experiences with GenAI-supported learning, cross-campus interaction, associated challenges, and recommendations for improving the learning experience. The findings indicate that students perceived GenAI as supporting concept clarification, greater perceived learning efficiency, idea development, and problem-solving, while also emphasizing the importance of critically evaluating GenAI-generated information. Cross-campus interaction was perceived as exposing students to diverse perspectives, experiences, and approaches to cybersecurity, which participants described as broadening their understanding of collaborative problem-solving across contexts. Students nevertheless identified challenges involving scheduling, technology, differences in experience, GenAI accuracy, and responsible use. They recommended more structured cross-campus projects, hands-on activities, clearer guidance on ethical GenAI use, and greater engagement with industry professionals. The study contributes to research on technology-enhanced learning in higher education by suggesting how GenAI-supported learning and geographically distributed peer interaction can provide complementary educational resources: GenAI offers immediate cognitive and informational support, while cross-campus interaction provides access to situated human perspectives. Together, these experiences highlight the importance of verification, contextual judgment, critical AI literacy, and intentional pedagogical design in GenAI-enhanced learning environments.

## Interactive Learning Environments (3편)

### 1. Navigating AI ethics in K–12 classrooms: exploring teachers’ AI ethics alignment through activity theory
- 저자: Huan Song, Runyi Lin, Kai Wang, Tong Wu  |  게재: 2026-11-05
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2754924](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2754924)

**초록:**
AI ethics in K–12 education remains conceptually visible but practically ambiguous: while teachers are expected to use AI responsibly, concrete boundaries for ethical classroom use remain largely undefined. Drawing on Activity Theory, this study examines how K–12 teachers in China understand AI ethics alignment and how the mediating elements of the activity system shape these understandings. Based on metaphor elicitation tasks completed by 26 teachers and semi-structured interviews with 16 of them, the study reveals mixed and still-developing perspectives on AI ethics. Their AI ethics alignment was shaped through the interaction of rules, tools, community, and division of labor. Teachers translated vague ethical expectations into provisional classroom boundaries, made situated judgments about the risks created by AI tools in classroom practice, and often acted as frontline gatekeepers when shared community norms and responsibility structures remained underdeveloped. The findings call for explicit school-level AI ethics protocols, stronger institutional support for teachers’ ethical judgment, and a more coordinated responsibility structure across teachers, school management, and AI developers.

### 2. Teaching presence, human–GenAI pedagogical partnership, and strategic AI-use regulation in cultural design learning: an instructional–relational–regulatory model
- 저자: Chia-Mei Lu, Shu Ching Yang, Chung–Ho Tien, Shih‐Chieh Hung  |  게재: 2026-11-04
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2754912](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2754912)

**초록:**
Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) raises questions about how instructional guidance, partnership perceptions, and regulation relate to students' learning experiences. This cross-sectional study examined an instructional-relational-regulatory model integrating teaching presence (TP), human-GenAI pedagogical partnership (HGP), strategic AI-use regulation (REG), GenAI-use engagement (GUE), cognitive load, and perceived cultural intelligence (CQ). Following a 14-day cultural field work and design activity in mainland China, the primary analysis included 912 students from 21 classes after excluding 32 straightlining responses. A 63-indicator higher-order structural equation model used WLSMV estimation and 2,000 department-stratified whole-class bootstrap draws. TP-HGP showed the largest standardized association (*β* = .695). HGP and REG were positively associated, and both were positively associated with GUE. REG and GUE, but not HGP, had positive conditional associations with perceived CQ. Extraneous cognitive load was negatively associated with REG, GUE, and perceived CQ; intrinsic-load associations were smaller. Results were generally stable across the full 944-case sample, MLR estimation, and leave-one-class-out analyses, although one weak intrinsic-load association was sample-sensitive. Method-component and alternative directional analyses did not eliminate common-method concerns or establish causal ordering. The findings distinguish relational positioning from self-reported regulatory enactment but do not demonstrate cultural-learning gains.

## Journal of Computer Assisted Learning (1편)

### 1. A Configurational Approach to Investigating the Use of Artificial Intelligence in Self‐Regulated Learning From Needs Satisfaction, Learning Environments and Perceived Affordances
- 저자: Xiu-Yi Wu, Qi Xia, Zubair Ahmad, Thomas K. F. Chiu  |  게재: 2026-11-05
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70371](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70371)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Integrating artificial intelligence (AI) tools into self‐regulated learning (SRL) is complex and multifaceted. Configurational approaches are based on the concept that combinations (or configurations) of causally relevant factors should be connected to the desired outcome. Objectives Self‐determination theory (SDT), the unified theory of acceptance and use of technology (UTAUT) and perceived affordances focus on basic needs, technology adoptions and technological designs, respectively. Taking a configurational approach, this study investigates how the combinations of SDT, UTAUT and perceived affordances affect SRL with AI. Methods We employed fuzzy‐set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) to uncover the complex interplay of factors that facilitate effective SRL. Data from 248 students reveal that a combination of autonomy satisfaction, relatedness satisfaction and performance expectancy is the most influential factor affecting the use of AI in SRL. Results and Conclusions Our results suggest that both innate psychological needs and external learning environments should be considered together for understanding the adoption of AI in SRL. They also further highlight the critical role of perceived affordances as mediators and moderators, enhancing the relationships between innate psychological needs and external learning environments. This integrative approach provides valuable insights into the design and implementation of AI‐supported SRL environments. We recommend designing the environments to foster autonomy and relatedness while also ensuring ease of use for learners.
