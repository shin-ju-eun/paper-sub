## Interactive Learning Environments (2편)

### 1. Expert evaluation of AI-assisted scenario design for interactive problem based learning environments in medical education
- 저자: Burcu Küçük Biçer, Yavuz Selim Kıyak, Özlem Çoşkun  |  게재: 2026-09-18
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2734312](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2734312)

**초록:**
Problem-based learning (PBL) is an interactive learning approach in which students develop understanding through collaborative inquiry, hypothesis generation, and interpretation of clinical information. Because scenario quality shapes reasoning and self-directed learning, this study examined whether AI-assisted scenario generation could support the development of high-quality PBL materials in medical education. Using a paired comparative quantitative design, human-written and ChatGPT-generated scenarios addressing identical learning objectives were compared. Twenty scenarios, organized as 10 matched pairs, were evaluated by independent academic experts using a 24-item checklist refined through pilot expert review. Across valid ratings, AI-generated scenarios received a higher proportion of positive evaluations than human-written scenarios (90.7% vs 80.3%), particularly for clarity, consistency, cognitive load balance, scientific accuracy, and support for differential diagnosis. Significant differences were observed in selected matched pairs after adjustment for multiple comparisons. Generative AI, when guided by explicit pedagogical principles and followed by expert review, may therefore serve as a useful co-design tool for PBL scenario development. Human academic judgment remains essential for psychosocial nuance, cultural sensitivity, and contextual depth.

### 2. GenAI dependency and psychological distress among Chinese university students: a cross-sectional examination of the serial mediating roles of fear of missing out and derailment
- 저자: Mingyue Cui, Xi Chen, Pu Sun, Ling Yan  |  게재: 2026-09-18
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2734315](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2734315)

**초록:**
As generative AI (GenAI) becomes increasingly embedded in higher education, concerns are growing about students’ dependency on these tools and its psychological correlates. This study examined the association between GenAI dependency and psychological distress among Chinese university students, tested the serial mediating roles of fear of missing out (FoMO) and derailment, and explored whether these pathways differed across cognitive–behavioral, functional, and emotional dependency. A convenience sample of 525 Chinese university students completed measures of GenAI dependency, FoMO, derailment, and psychological distress, including depression, anxiety, and stress. Structural equation modeling and bias-corrected percentile bootstrap analyses showed that GenAI dependency was positively associated with psychological distress. FoMO and derailment each showed significant mediating effects, and their serial mediation was also significant. Dimension-specific analyses indicated that cognitive–behavioral dependency exhibited indirect links to distress through both FoMO and derailment. Functional dependency showed significant specific indirect pathways involving FoMO, although its total indirect effect was not significant. Emotional dependency showed no significant unique mediating pathway in the dimension-specific model. These findings suggest that the association between GenAI dependency and psychological distress may involve both affective–cognitive concerns and disruptions in self-continuity, with cognitive–behavioral dependency representing a particularly salient risk-related pattern.

### 1. The Effects of a Computerised Teacher‐Mediated Intervention on the Emotional Understanding of Autistic Children
- 저자: Ifat Bar, Ofer Golan, Sigal Eden  |  게재: 2026-09-17
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70332](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70332)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Autism spectrum disorder is associated with persistent challenges in emotional understanding, encompassing difficulties in recognising emotions from nonverbal cues, interpreting others' affective and mental states, as well as in emotional language. These challenges may impede the social functioning of autistic children. Objectives This study evaluated the effectiveness of EmotiPlay, a computer‐based, research‐supported intervention designed to enhance emotion understanding, which was adapted into a teacher‐mediated format for autistic children in special education classrooms. Methods A total of 116 autistic children (17 girls), aged 7–10, were randomly assigned at the class level to either an intervention group ( n = 59), which received two EmotiPlay lessons per week for 22 weeks or a control group ( n = 57), which maintained a standard special education curriculum. Outcome measures included emotion recognition from nonverbal cues, cognitive empathy and emotional language. Social functioning was additionally assessed through observation during free play. Results and Conclusions Compared to the control group, children in the intervention group showed significant improvements in interpreting emotions from nonverbal cues and social context, as well as in their emotional language. However, these improvements did not generalise to emotions beyond the programme or to broader social behaviours. These findings demonstrate the potential and limitations of integrating computer‐based interventions targeting socio‐emotional learning in special education settings. Trial Registration: ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05902832
