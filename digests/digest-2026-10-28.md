## International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education (2편)

### 1. Who benefits from LLMs? Prompt engineering competence and equity in higher education
- 저자: Zhengdao Li, Yezi Liu, Jianan Jiang, Zhenxin Zhang, Zhihao Li et al.  |  게재: 2026-10-26
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00633-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00633-6)

**초록:**
Abstract Large language models (LLMs) are rapidly becoming embedded in higher education, yet their benefits may be unevenly distributed. This study conceptualizes prompt engineering competence (PEC) as an equity-relevant interaction competence and examines whether differences in PEC are associated with perceived academic gains and perceived educational inequity. We adopt a two-stage quantitative design combining a cross-sectional survey with a within-subject prompt-quality experiment. Stage 1 reports a survey of Chinese undergraduates who have used LLMs ( N = 380), measuring PEC, perceived academic performance gains, perceived educational inequity, family digital capital, gaming experience, and a planning/independent-work tendency. Regression and mediation analyses show that PEC was strongly associated with perceived academic gains, while its association with perceived inequity was statistically significant but small. Background resources and learning-related experiences and dispositions explained substantial variance in PEC, especially gaming experience and planning/independent-work tendency, and the association between family digital capital and perceived academic gains was largely indirect through PEC. Stage 2 conducts a within-subject prompt-quality experiment across four higher-education task types and four popular models, comparing basic prompts with a student-oriented five-component refined prompt framework (Role–Goal–Input–Process–Output). Analysis of 400 matched prompt comparisons, comprising 800 model outputs, indicates that the refined prompt scaffold consistently outperforms basic prompts. Overall, the findings identify PEC as a potential competence pathway through which students may differ in their ability to convert LLM access into learning-related benefits, while structured prompt scaffolds may provide a scalable approach to reducing prompt-quality barriers in LLM-supported higher education.

### 2. GenAI-enabled educational game development: the effects of a scripted mystery role-playing game on students’ learning experience and learning outcomes in legal education
- 저자: Ziyi Xie, Zhao Shufang, Fanxiang Hong, Zhizhuang Duan  |  게재: 2026-10-27
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00634-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00634-5)

**초록:**
Abstract Although previous studies have shown that GenAI can support learning engagement and motivation, it remains unclear how GenAI-assisted development of offline educational games based on Scripted Mystery Role-Playing Games (SMRPG) influences students’ learning experiences and outcomes. Based on the SMRPG format, this study developed an offline educational game with the assistance of Gemini 3 Pro to explore its potential value for students’ learning outcomes and experiences in higher education contexts. A sequential explanatory mixed-methods design was employed with 84 undergraduate students majoring in law as participants. First, a randomized controlled experiment compared learning outcomes between an experimental group ( N = 42) and a control group ( N = 42). Subsequently, qualitative interviews were conducted with 24 students who participated in the game to examine their learning experiences during the gameplay process. Quantitative results showed that, after controlling for pre-test scores, the experimental group achieved significantly higher post-test scores than the control group (F(1, 81) = 105.61, p <.001, partial η² = 0.566). Qualitative findings indicate that participants described an immersive learning experience involving reasoning scenarios and role interaction in the GenAI-assisted game. This experience was associated with greater willingness to engage in learning, provided opportunities for the mobilization and application of relevant knowledge, and supported sustained motivation and reflective learning. This study provides empirical evidence on the learning experiences and learning outcomes associated with applying GenAI to develop legal educational games based on SMRPG, offering insights for future research and practice on GenAI-enabled educational game development.

## Journal of Computer Assisted Learning (1편)

### 1. When Do Learning Habits Matter? Linking Behavioural Stages to Academic Performance Using Longitudinal Log Data
- 저자: Chia-Yu Hsu, Hiroaki Ogata  |  게재: 2026-10-26
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70364](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70364)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Developing effective learning habits—such as studying at regular times—can significantly influence academic performance. While prior research has shown that structured routines benefit learners, most studies rely on self‐reported surveys and provide only a snapshot of behaviour change. Little is known about how learning habits evolve over time or how these shifts affect learning outcomes in technology‐enhanced environments. Addressing this gap is critical for designing personalised, timely support that adapts to learners' behavioural states. Objectives This study investigates how learners progress through different stages of learning habit formation and how these stages relate to academic performance over time. Methods This study analysed 3 years of daily learning log data from 120 Japanese junior high school students. The data were collected through a school‐wide digital learning environment that integrates a learning management system, a digital e‐book system, and a self‐regulated/directed learning support tool. Grounded in the transtheoretical model of behaviour change, this study extracted learners' monthly habit stages from the log data and examined their relationship with exam performance across nine mathematics assessments. Results and Conclusions Academic performance improved significantly as students reached the ‘preparation’ stage, marking a transition from irregular exploratory behaviours to regular weekly study within a single month. However, reaching the ‘maintenance’ stage—the highest stage in the transtheoretical model, representing the culmination of habit‐building through consistent weekly study for at least 4 months—led to performance stabilisation rather than further gains, suggesting a plateau effect once behaviours become automatic. Notably, some learners maintained their academic levels even after regressing to lower habit stages, providing evidence of a residual effect where the cognitive benefits of previously established habits persist despite behavioural fluctuations. This study contributes a novel method for identifying when to offer timely support and suggests how digital learning tools can adapt based on students' habit stages.
