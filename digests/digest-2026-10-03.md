## Computers & Education (1편)

### 1. How we study what we study: A methodological review of 50 years of educational technology research
- 저자: Jo Tondeur, Sarah Katherine Howard, Antonio Bucchiarone, Ronny Scherer, Johan van Braak  |  게재: 2026-10-01
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2026.105760](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2026.105760)

**초록:**
Research in educational technology has been extensively mapped in terms of what has been studied, but far less attention has been paid to how it has been studied. This paper reports a methodological review of fifty years of research in Computers & Education, covering a corpus of 5,516 articles published between 1976 and 2026, with the principal findings based on the post-1996 full-text corpus. Using an AI-assisted analysis, applied to full texts and abstracts, we traced the evolution of research paradigms, approaches, and methods across the journal's full publication history. The principal findings are based on the post-1996 full-text corpus. Pre-1996, full-text machine-readable files were not available for analysis. Analysis of pre-1996 abstracts was possible and are included to provide context for trends observed post-1996. The findings show that quantitative approaches and questionnaire-based data collection have constituted the field's sustained methodological centre of gravity. Within this dominant pattern, two shifts stand out: 1) the rise of systematic reviews as a recognised approach, and 2) a computational turn. The range of methods used in research has grown steadily, suggesting a gradual broadening of the field's repertoire. These patterns are only visible across multiple decades. Findings reveal that quantitative dominance is not a recent development but a persistent structural feature, and that the computational surge is better understood as a second wave following a peak in the mid-2000s. These findings have implications for what kinds of knowledge educational technology research is equipped to produce. The study argues that understanding a field's methodological history is a prerequisite for extending it.

## Journal of Computer Assisted Learning (1편)

### 1. Generative AI ‐Supported Creative Writing Among Primary School Students: A Study of Creative Writing Performance, Writing Self‐Efficacy, and Flow State
- 저자: Xin Liu, Yi Wen, Xiaozhe Yang, Yihe Gao  |  게재: 2026-10-02
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70347](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70347)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background The integration of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) into creative writing is a growing trend. However, few studies have examined primary school students' interactions with GenAI alongside their writing experiences and resulting writing products. Objectives This study compared creative writing performance, writing self‐efficacy, and flow state across GenAI‐supported, peer‐collaborative and individual writing conditions. It also descriptively examined patterns of student–AI interaction during the writing activity. Methods An experiment involved 78 primary school students in China assigned to three conditions: GenAI‐supported writing ( n = 20), peer collaboration ( n = 38; 19 pairs), and individual writing ( n = 20). Results and Conclusions Students in the GenAI‐supported writing group obtained higher creative writing performance scores than those in the peer‐collaborative and individual writing groups. Interaction analysis showed that students mainly used GenAI to generate and elaborate ideas. Among episodes with observable uptake, adapted or selective incorporation was more common than direct incorporation, and no complete GenAI response was reproduced verbatim. In addition, students in the GenAI‐supported writing group also reported higher writing self‐efficacy than those in the individual writing group, although the difference from the peer‐collaborative writing group was not significant. However, they reported the lowest flow state. Writing self‐efficacy was positively associated with creative writing performance, whereas no significant association between flow state and creative writing performance was observed in this sample. These findings suggest that GenAI may serve as a resource for developing ideas in primary school creative writing.
