## Interactive Learning Environments (2편)

### 1. Construction of an evaluation index system for graduate students’ effective generative AI utilization ability: a meta-intelligence perspective
- 저자: Xiaolong Qian, Junfu Dong, Shiying Yin, Beibei Huang  |  게재: 2026-09-29
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2735923](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2735923)

**초록:**
The incorporation of generative AI into higher education has created new opportunities and challenges for graduate students' research and learning. This study developed an evaluation index system for graduate students’ effective generative AI utilization ability based on meta-intelligence theory and literature analysis. A two-round Delphi consultation with 17 experts was conducted to refine the initial indicators, followed by a questionnaire survey of 478 graduate students. Exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis were conducted to examine the structural validity of the system. The results supported a four-dimensional structure comprising analytical ability, creative ability, practical ability, and wisdom ability, including 19 secondary indicators and 46 specific items. The four-factor model demonstrated satisfactory fit, with standardized factor loadings exceeding 0.7 and strong reliability and validity evidence. Subjective and objective weighting methods were further integrated to determine indicator weights. This study provides a theoretically grounded and empirically supported basis for evaluating and cultivating graduate students’ effective generative AI utilization ability.

### 1. From Digital Competence to Collaborative Knowledge Construction: The Role of Social Presence in Online Learning Environments
- 저자: Fahimeh Keshavarzi, Elham Heidari, Zahra Rafatjoo, Ghasem Salimi, Mohammadreza Farrokhnia  |  게재: 2026-09-29
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70348](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70348)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Collaborative knowledge construction (CKC) is an important process in online higher education, requiring students to communicate, share information, and collectively develop knowledge through digital environments. Although digital competence (DC) may be relevant to these processes, CKC also depends on the social and interpersonal conditions reflected in social presence (SP). However, limited research has examined how DC and SP jointly relate to CKC in online learning. Objective This study examined the relationships among university students' perceived DC, SP, and CKC and investigated whether SP accounted for part of the relationship between DC and CKC in online learning environments. Methods A total of 341 students at a public university in Iran reported on their overall synchronous online‐learning experiences during the 2024–2025 academic year. DC encompassed information and data literacy, communication and collaboration, and digital content creation, while SP included affective expression, open communication, and group cohesion. Structural equation modelling was used to examine the proposed relationships, and bootstrapping was employed to assess the indirect association between DC and CKC through SP. Results Perceived DC was positively associated with CKC ( β = 0.27, p < 0.001) and SP ( β = 0.47, p < 0.001), while SP was positively associated with CKC ( β = 0.61, p < 0.001). The model explained 21.6% of the variance in SP and 60.2% of the variance in CKC. DC also showed a significant indirect association with CKC through SP ( β = 0.29, 95% BC CI [0.21, 0.38]), while the direct association remained significant, a pattern consistent with partial statistical mediation. Conclusions The findings suggest that the relationship between perceived DC and CKC is partly socially embedded. Perceived DC was related to CKC both directly and indirectly through SP, indicating that digital capabilities and interpersonal conditions may jointly matter for online CKC. Online learning may therefore benefit from combining support for students' DC with collaborative activities that foster meaningful interaction, interpersonal connection, and collective knowledge development.

### 2. The Impact of Language Proficiency on MOOC Performance: Insights From Video Interactions and Forum Participation
- 저자: Tanguy Dubois, Damien De Meyere, Patrick Watrin, Magali Paquot  |  게재: 2026-09-29
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70338](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70338)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Massive Open Online Courses or MOOCs have the potential to democratise education by making high‐quality learning opportunities accessible to students from diverse socioeconomic backgrounds and geographical locations. However, many courses on major platforms like EdX are offered exclusively in English, limiting accessibility for non‐native speakers. Beyond the simple native/non‐native divide, it remains unknown how different levels of English proficiency influence learners' performance in MOOCs. Objectives Our study investigates how language proficiency influences performance in English‐taught MOOCs and modulates the effect of participants' interactions with videos and discussion forums. Methods We retrieved the self‐assessed language proficiency from the pre‐ and post‐course surveys of 1268 participants for two MOOCs on EdX, viz., International Law and International Humanitarian Law. To capture these participants' interactions with videos and discussion forums, we generated measures of their use of pauses, closed captions, video playback rate, forum posts, etc. based on log data gathered automatically by EdX. Using mixed‐effects beta‐binomial regression, we relate these variables to their performance, calculated as the score they obtained on multiple‐choice questions throughout the course. Results and Conclusions In line with our expectations, interactions with discussion forums and especially videos have a positive effect on the performance of low‐proficiency participants, which could help them overcome the language barrier they face compared to more proficient and native participants. However, the majority of low‐proficiency participants do not make sufficient use of these video and discussion‐forum functionalities to fully benefit from their potential.
