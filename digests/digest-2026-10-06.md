## International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education (1편)

### 1. Seeing what students learn: assessing cognitive representations and academic outcomes via EEG image reconstruction
- 저자: Zhu Su, Zhiqing Ouyang, Xiaoxuan Shao, Xinchen Huang, Sannyuya Liu et al.  |  게재: 2026-10-05
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00626-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00626-5)

**초록:**
Abstract Understanding how humans process visual information during learning remains a central challenge in cognitive neuroscience. Traditional approaches typically rely on behavioral observations or indirect neural measures, which limits direct insight into learners’ internal cognitive representations. Recent advances in brain–computer interfaces (BCIs) and deep learning have enabled image reconstruction from electroencephalography (EEG), providing a promising pathway to decode latent cognitive states. In this study, we investigate how EEG-based image reconstruction relates to students’ learning. While students watched educational videos, we recorded EEG signals and used an image reconstruction model to generate images reflecting their perceived visual representations. We assessed reconstruction quality using semantic similarity between reconstructed and original images. First, we examine whether semantic similarity is associated with overall academic performance and find a significant positive association in our sample. Second, we evaluate item-level effects and show that questions answered incorrectly correspond to significantly lower semantic similarity than correctly answered questions. Third, we analyze the temporal dynamics of reconstruction and demonstrate that temporal misalignment patterns—quantified through dispersion-related measures—are associated with learners’ performance. Overall, these exploratory results provide preliminary evidence that EEG-based image reconstruction is sensitive to both the strength and the temporal evolution of learners’ cognitive representations. If replicated in larger and more diverse samples, this approach may offer a promising direction for studying learning processes and for informing more personalized, data-driven educational interventions.

## Interactive Learning Environments (1편)

### 1. Comparing chatbot and peer interaction among trilingual students: effects on self-regulated learning, critical thinking, task engagement and reading comprehension
- 저자: Huiling Ma  |  게재: 2026-10-05
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2743759](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2743759)

**초록:**
This study examined the differential effects of chatbot-mediated interaction and peer interaction on trilingual students’ English reading comprehension, self-regulated learning, critical thinking, and task engagement. Using a mixed-methods design, the study combined pre- and post-intervention quantitative measures with semi-structured interviews conducted throughout the intervention. Quantitative findings revealed that both instructional conditions significantly improved reading comprehension, with no significant between-group difference after controlling for baseline performance. After controlling for baseline performance, the peer interaction group demonstrated significantly higher task engagement than the chatbot-mediated group. The chatbot-mediated group demonstrated significant within-group improvement in self-regulated learning; however, no significant between-group difference was observed. Critical thinking scores increased modestly in both groups, but the within-group changes were not statistically significant, and no significant between-group difference was found. Qualitative analysis identified distinct interactional affordances and constraints. Peer interaction was associated with collaborative engagement, exposure to diverse perspectives, and shared meaning-making, whereas chatbot-mediated interaction provided relatively low-pressure practice, individualized feedback, and opportunities for learner regulation, while presenting challenges related to emotional responsiveness and discourse coherence. Overall, chatbot-mediated and peer interaction were associated with distinct learning experiences and outcomes during reading tasks. The findings highlight their potentially complementary pedagogical roles in trilingual English reading contexts.

## Journal of Research on Technology in Education (2편)

### 1. Generative AI and the balance of effort in group tasks: Appropriate, over-, and under-scaffolding
- 저자: Anna Korchak, Christopher Hughes, Jamie Costley  |  게재: 2026-10-05
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2026.2724351](https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2026.2724351)

**초록:**
Generative artificial intelligence (Gen-AI) is increasingly used in higher education, yet its role in collaborative learning remains unclear. This study examines how students use Gen-AI during group lesson-planning tasks in an undergraduate education course. Drawing on Vygotsky’s sociocultural theory, we introduce the concept of “an effort space” and apply the categories of appropriate, over-, and under-scaffolding to Gen-AI use. The analysis of 75 students’ reflections, chat logs, and lesson plans identified three patterns: appropriate support that preserved learner effort, over-scaffolding through task outsourcing or free-riding that eliminates learner effort, and under-scaffolding through AI avoidance. These patterns show how Gen-AI reshapes collaborative learning.

### 2. Effective communication skills in simulated practice experiences for preservice teachers
- 저자: Logan W. Qualls, Shanna Eisner Hirsch, Catherine A. Griffith, Samantha N. Scott, Ekemini Eshiett  |  게재: 2026-10-05
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2026.2736121](https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2026.2736121)

**초록:**
Effective collaboration is essential to supporting the diverse needs of students with disabilities. Teachers must collaborate effectively by communicating with other professionals and families to create accessible instruction; however, few opportunities exist for preservice teachers to practice communicating effectively. Promising findings have been reported for simulated practice involving instruction; however, little is known about simulated practice involving effective communication. We conducted a conceptual replication, utilizing a randomized pretest-posttest design to compare the effects of two practice experiences: mixed reality simulation and peer roleplay. Participants included 31 preservice teachers in an introduction to special education course. Across groups, participants increased their communication skills, significant differences between groups at post-test were revealed, and a cost-effectiveness analysis was conducted. We discuss implications for both teacher educators and researchers.
