## Journal of Research on Technology in Education (2편)

### 1. Unpacking interaction profiles and strategies in human-AI collaborative problem solving: a cognitive distribution and regulation perspective
- 저자: Zhanxin Hao, Xiaobo Liu, Jiaxin Fan, Yun Long, Jifan Yu et al.  |  게재: 2026-08-29
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2026.2722962](https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2026.2722962)

**초록:**
This study adopts an integrated distributed cognition and regulation of learning perspective to examine the collaboration patterns and dynamics of human-AI interaction when college students collaborate with AI for complex problem-solving. Through cluster analysis, three distinct collaborative problem-solving modes were identified in this study: Delegated Reasoning (DR), Concerted Interpretation (CI), and Delegated Elaboration (DE). This study found that the DR group achieved the highest task performance, significantly outperforming the CI group. Additionally, the semantic similarity between human and AI discourse was notably the highest in the DR group. In contrast, the CI group reported significantly greater use of self-regulation strategies. These findings uncover a critical tension between the efficiency of the distributed system and the depth of human learners’ regulatory engagement. Insights from this study offer valuable implications for the future design of AI-empowered educational tools and student-AI collaborative learning frameworks.

### 2. Transforming artificial intelligence from technical innovation to pedagogical practice in schools: Educational leadership perspective
- 저자: Mohammad Noman, Jie Chen, Tong Lin  |  게재: 2026-08-29
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2026.2724352](https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2026.2724352)

**초록:**
This study draws on in-depth case studies of six primary schools in Zhejiang Province to examine how principals and teachers perceive and respond to the integration of artificial intelligence in education. Using interviews and document analysis, the findings show that leaders approach AI with cautious optimism, valuing its potential to streamline administration and support instruction while remaining attentive to pedagogical, ethical, and relational risks. Principals paced the adoption of this approach as a form of care, distributed responsibility to teachers, and acted as ethical guardians of classroom practice. Teachers emphasized reassurance, infrastructure, and professional learning as central to meaningful engagement. The study extends leadership theory by demonstrating how leadership makes artificial intelligence educational, rather than purely technical, by mediating between policy ambitions and local realities.
