## International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education (1편)

### 1. ChatGPT’s feedback credibility and students’ perceived learning: the role of agent-related social perceptions
- 저자: Raúl Pérez‐López, Rubén González-Vallejo, Laura Lucia‐Palacios, Iguácel Melero‐Polo, Andreea Trifu  |  게재: 2026-10-14
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00629-2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00629-2)

**초록:**
Abstract This paper examines whether students’ agent-related social perceptions of ChatGPT are associated with the perceived credibility of its feedback in academic tasks and whether such credibility, in turn, is related to students’ perceived learning. It also investigates whether the relationship between feedback credibility and perceived learning varies according to students’ sensitivity to the quality of their academic work and their sensitivity to grade-based rewards. To address this, we develop a theoretical model grounded in social perception theory and prior research on students’ experiences with AI-based tools. The model is tested using Structural Equation Modelling (SEM) on data collected from 230 students who completed an academic writing task. The findings reveal that students’ agent-related social perceptions—particularly perceived competence and human-like empathy—are positively connected to the credibility students attribute to its feedback. In turn, perceived feedback credibility is interestingly related to students’ perceived learning in the context of academic writing. Furthermore, the positive link between credibility and perceived learning is weaker among students with higher grade-based reward sensitivity. This research contributes to the educational technology literature by advancing understanding of how students evaluate the credibility of ChatGPT-generated feedback and how such evaluations are associated with learning perceptions. It also shows that agent-related social perceptions play a significant role in students’ credibility judgements. Finally, the findings offer practical implications for educators and students seeking to integrate ChatGPT effectively into academic activities.

## Interactive Learning Environments (1편)

### 1. ChatGPT as cognitive scaffold: computational thinking and metacognition in AI-embedded instruction
- 저자: Tanya Chichekian, Joel Trudeau  |  게재: 2026-10-13
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2744894](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2744894)

**초록:**
Drawing on a computational thinking model and cognitive offloading theory, this exploratory mixed-methods study examined whether structured AI scaffolding is associated with differential computational thinking (CT) outcomes and explored the role of metacognitive self-regulation. Two naturally occurring sections (n = 47) of the same liberal arts course, taught by the same instructor in consecutive Fall semesters, one embedding ChatGPT and one not, were compared using independent-samples t-tests, multiple regression, and inductive content analysis. Students in the ChatGPT-embedded section reported significantly higher problem-solving scores on a validated CT instrument. Digital literacy scores did not differ, possibly reflecting measurement limitations or the intervention's focus on reasoning over general digital fluency. Problem solving correlated significantly with metacognitive strategy use and contributed to a significant overall regression model, but did not remain a unique predictor after adjusting for digital literacy and class membership. Qualitative analysis identified three themes: ChatGPT as a cognitive scaffold, ChatGPT as a cognitive risk, and student-initiated boundary-setting. Most students expressed all three simultaneously, suggesting structured AI-embedded environments may foster metacognitive vigilance alongside CT gains. Findings support deliberate instructional design and explicit metacognitive supports when integrating ChatGPT in higher education.

## Journal of Computer Assisted Learning (1편)

### 1. Desktop Virtual Reality for Public First‐Aid Training: Effects on Cognitive Load and Learning Outcomes
- 저자: Yang Wang, Jia Li  |  게재: 2026-10-13
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70340](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70340)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Public first‐aid education must help learners apply procedural knowledge in emergencies, yet conventional instruction offers limited contextualized practice. Desktop virtual reality (DVR) can provide interactive, scenario‐based learning, but evidence regarding its effects on cognitive load and cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) knowledge is inconclusive. Objectives Our study compared DVR‐based and video‐based CPR instruction in terms of learners' cognitive load and performance on CPR knowledge. Methods Eighty‐four university students were randomly allocated to DVR or video instruction. Complete‐case analyses included 64 participants (32 per condition) with valid cognitive‐load, pre‐test and post‐test data. Between‐group differences were examined using two‐sided independent‐samples t ‐tests and Mann–Whitney U tests; interviews with six DVR participants were analysed deductively to contextualize their experiences. Results and Conclusions The DVR group reported lower overall cognitive load ( p = 0.010) and lower scores on both prespecified cognitive load dimensions, cognitive behavioural assessment ( p = 0.033) and cognitive efficiency assessment ( p = 0.015), than the video group. At post‐test, DVR participants performed better on the four‐item CPR detail set ( p = 0.038), while the groups did not differ on basic items. The tested DVR condition, which combined signal guidance, spatial visualization, multisensory stimulation, self‐paced progression, scenario simulation and prompt feedback, was associated with lower reported cognitive load and better performance on the CPR detail knowledge than video instruction, while performance on the basic knowledge did not differ. These findings support the use of DVR as a structured supplement to CPR instruction, enabling learners to rehearse spatial and procedural relations at their own pace and receive prompt feedback. They also suggest that designers should evaluate guidance, navigation, feedback and scenario features as an integrated instructional configuration.
