## Interactive Learning Environments (3편)

### 1. Design affordance diagnostic framework: screening construct-irrelevant variance in computational thinking assessment
- 저자: Jewoong Moon, Tugce Aldemir, Gyuri Byun  |  게재: 2026-09-15
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2730634](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2730634)

**초록:**
Interactive virtual makerspaces generate process data, but platform affordances can complicate their interpretation as evidence of computational thinking (CT). This exploratory study presents the Design Affordance Diagnostic Framework (DADF), which evaluates indicators through five gates: variance, reliability, association with outcomes, robustness to non-independence and multiple-testing control. Artifacts from two cohorts of pre-service teachers completing a Roblox game design activity were examined. Using LLM-coded CT outcomes, network density showed a positive unadjusted association in 2024 but was not statistically significant when pooled (r = .16, n = 55). Reflection depth showed a positive pooled association (r = .36, n = 55) and remained provisional because of coding reliability limitations. In 2025, logic density showed a positive unadjusted association (r = .40, n = 25) and was retained as provisional; code volume was not statistically significant (r = −.18, n = 25). Available revised-analysis records do not establish cluster-robust or multiplicity-adjusted inference, so full passage through all gates is not established. DADF provides a procedure for identifying indicators that require further validation across cohorts, instruments and platforms.

### 2. Transforming teacher professional identity through interactive digital communities of practice: insights from language teachers in Indonesia
- 저자: Frida Akmalia, Erna Andriyanti, Sulis Triyono  |  게재: 2026-09-16
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2734313](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2734313)

**초록:**
Interactive digital professional learning has become increasingly important in supporting teachers’ continuous professional development; however, relatively little is known about how participation in digital communities of practice contributes to professional identity development within language-specific teaching contexts. This qualitative case study explored how participation in an interactive digital professional learning community contributed to the professional identity development of 25 Arabic language teachers working in Indonesian Madrasah Tsanawiyah (MTs). Data were collected over four months through semi-structured interviews, Google Classroom discussion archives, WhatsApp interaction records, online observations, and researcher field notes, and analysed using reflexive thematic analysis. The findings identified four interconnected processes: professional belonging, collaborative reflection and pedagogical transformation, professional role repositioning, and teacher agency. These processes developed through sustained collaborative participation, reflective dialogue, peer recognition, and engagement with shared professional practice, rather than through technology use alone. The findings suggest that digital communities become consequential for professional identity development when teachers can engage with shared pedagogical concerns, contribute professional knowledge, and translate collaborative learning into professional action. The study contributes to research on interactive learning environments by providing a contextually grounded account of the participatory processes connecting digital professional learning with teacher professional identity development in a language-specific community.

## Journal of Computer Assisted Learning (1편)

### 1. The Complexity of Visual Learning Materials Moderates the Effects of Instructors' Facial Expressions in Instructional Videos
- 저자: Xuemei Huang, Yuan Yang, Xin Zhao, Yuxian Ma, Zhongling Pi et al.  |  게재: 2026-09-15
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70336](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70336)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Research shows that instructors' happy facial expressions can evoke students' positive emotions and enhance their learning motivation. Nevertheless, debate persists in the literature regarding the impact of instructors' happy facial expressions on students' learning performance. This may be attributed to the potential social benefits of instructors' happy facial expressions being counterbalanced by the concurrent attentional costs. Objectives This study examined whether the visual complexity of learning materials (simple vs. complex) moderated the effect of instructors' facial expressions (happy vs. bored) on students' learning performance. Methods Participants ( n = 58) watched two instructional videos featuring an instructor displaying either happy or bored facial expressions while presenting either complex or simple visual materials. Result and Conclusions The experimental results revealed that, when simple visual learning materials were used, the instructor's happy facial expressions significantly improved students' learning performance. In contrast, when complex visual learning materials were used, differences in the instructor's facial expressions did not significantly affect students' learning performance. Implications Our findings suggest that instructors should consider both the complexity of the visual learning materials and their facial expressions when teaching English vocabulary as a foreign language through instructional videos, particularly when teaching female students. Specifically, when using simple visual learning materials, instructors should display happy facial expressions to enhance student performance. Conversely, when using complex visual learning materials, instructors should avoid overly expressive facial expressions that may distract students from the learning content.

## Education and Information Technologies (2편)

### 1. Extending the theory of planned behaviour to explain contract cheating in the age of generative AI: evidence from Australian higher education
- 저자: Madugoda Gunaratnege Senali  |  게재: 2026-09-15
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-026-14145-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-026-14145-3)

**초록:**
Abstract Academic contract cheating (CC) poses a significant challenge for Higher Education (HE), a problem that has become more complex with the increasing use of Generative Artificial Intelligence (Gen AI) tools. While similarity detection software can flag AI-generated text, it remains ineffective in identifying CC, potentially displacing misconduct rather than reducing it. Drawing on and extending the Theory of Planned Behaviour (TPB), this study examines how traditional TPB constructs and technology-mediated perceptual factors jointly influence students’ intentions and behaviours related to CC within the Australian HE context. The findings reveal significant direct and indirect pathways linking perceived instructor involvement, detection awareness, AI-related risk perceptions, and perceived behavioural control to students’ intentions and behaviours. These results extend the TPB framework to the context of AI-mediated academic misconduct and offer practical implications for universities in Australia and potentially other similar contexts. The study provides actionable insights for HE institutions to design integrity policies, assessment strategies, detection mechanisms, and digital literacy initiatives to strengthen academic integrity in alignment with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) on Quality Education (Goal 4).
