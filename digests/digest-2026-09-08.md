### 1. Bridging the Gap: Investigating Students' Self‐Reported AI ‐Literacy and Perceived Employability Competencies for an AI ‐Enabled Workforce
- 저자: Areej ElSayary, Karim Ragab  |  게재: 2026-09-07
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70327](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70327)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into the global economy requires higher education institutions to equip graduates with AI literacy. However, there is limited empirical research on the relationship between AI literacy and employability, particularly within the rapidly transforming context of the Middle East and Gulf regions. Objectives This study aimed to: (1) assess the self‐reported AI literacy and perceived employability competencies of university students in the Middle East and Gulf regions; (2) examine the associations between specific dimensions of self‐reported AI literacy and perceived employability; and (3) investigate whether students' attitudes and mindsets were statistically involved in theoretically ordered indirect associations between their self‐reported AI literacy and perceived employability outcomes. Methods A quantitative, cross‐sectional survey was conducted with 380 university students recruited via the Prolific platform. In the final analytic file, country of residence was retained only as two pooled codes: the Middle East ( n = 284; 74.7%) and the Gulf regions ( n = 96; 25.3%). Following within‐sample refinement, the instrument retained four dimensions of self‐reported AI literacy and eight composites of perceived employability competencies. Data were analysed using hierarchical multiple regression and Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS‐SEM). Results Mean levels of self‐reported AI literacy and perceived employability competencies were high. The full regression model, including five background controls, accounted for 60.8% of the variance in overall perceived employability readiness (the four self‐reported AI‐literacy dimensions contributed an increment of 55.4 percentage points; Δ R 2 = 0.554), with the ‘Analyze & Evaluate’ dimension showing the strongest standardised association. Attitudes & Mindsets composite was statistically involved in significant, theoretically ordered indirect associations between two self‐reported AI‐literacy dimensions (Understand & Knowledge; Use & Application/Create & Innovate) and all eight perceived employability outcomes. In the regression models, ‘Analyse & Evaluate’ was associated with both outcomes, overall perceived employability readiness and perceived career readiness. All reported outcomes are students' self‐assessments; they do not represent objectively demonstrated AI competence, employer‐rated competencies, actual employment outcomes, or temporal and causal order. Conclusions In this cross‐sectional sample, all four self‐reported AI‐related dimensions were positively associated with perceived employability readiness; attitudes and mindsets were statistically involved in indirect association patterns for two of the three antecedent dimensions. However, the observed association between critical thinking and the analytical and evaluative dimensions of AI literacy does not demonstrate that a direct pedagogical focus develops critical thinking. The findings provide a bounded rationale for further evaluating how AI‐literacy learning opportunities may be designed and assessed in comparable settings.

### 2. Utilising Gamification With Badges and Leaderboards in a Secondary EFL Course: Effects on Student Interest, Self‐Efficacy, Performance and Gender Differences
- 저자: Chunqi Li, Yuchun Zhong, Luke K. Fryer, Alex Shum  |  게재: 2026-09-07
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70320](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70320)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Properly designed badges and leaderboards may support English learning by fostering interest and self‐efficacy. Badges are visual symbols acknowledging individual achievements. Leaderboards display one's performance relative to others. Prior research has suggested that gender may influence students' perceptions of badges and leaderboards and their subsequent learning outcomes, although the findings are not universally consistent. Despite growing attention to potential gender differences in gamification research, few studies have examined how gender influenced the effects of gamification using badges and leaderboards on English learning. Objectives This mixed‐methods study examined the impact of gamification with badges and leaderboards on Chinese secondary students' interest, self‐efficacy and performance in learning English as a foreign language (EFL) and whether the effects differed by gender. Methods This 3‐week study recruited 396 first‐year secondary school students. After excluding participants with invalid or no responses ( n = 23), the final sample size comprised 373 students (males = 195; females = 178). Results The gamification intervention enhanced students' interest and self‐efficacy, but did not significantly affect their performance compared to non‐gamification. The effects of gamification were similar for boys and girls, and most students reported positive perceptions of the gamified course. Conclusions Using gamification with badges and leaderboards is a cost‐efficient approach to support English learning interest and self‐efficacy in formal education. Future studies should implement gamification over longer periods and investigate the effects of removing it to better understand the nonsignificant finding of performance. Moreover, future studies should investigate student perceptions of badges and leaderboards in greater depth.
