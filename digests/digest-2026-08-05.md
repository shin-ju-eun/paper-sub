## Interactive Learning Environments (2편)

### 1. Designing interactive industry–education learning environments: technology integration, knowledge transformation, self-efficacy, and innovation readiness
- 저자: Yanan Yu, Zhaoxia Huang, Yungwei Hao  |  게재: 2026-08-03
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2699326](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2699326)

**초록:**
As higher education emphasizes interdisciplinary innovation, industry–education integrated instruction and interactive learning technologies have become important approaches for practical learning. Yet less is known about how such environments shape innovation readiness. This study examined whether complex knowledge transformation and self-efficacy explained the relationship between technology-integrated industry–education learning environments and innovation readiness. A quasi-experimental mixed-methods design was conducted in a university capstone course. Data included surveys, process records, performance assessments, and interviews. Because students were assigned through existing enrollment procedures rather than randomization, the findings are interpreted as relational rather than causal evidence. Results showed that the instructional model was associated with complex knowledge transformation and self-efficacy. Complex knowledge transformation was related to self-efficacy and innovation readiness. Structural modeling showed no significant direct path from the instructional model to innovation readiness, but significant indirect paths through complex knowledge transformation and self-efficacy. Multi-group analysis showed that self-efficacy was more strongly related to innovation readiness among students with higher knowledge transformation. These findings suggest that technology-integrated industry–education learning environments support innovation readiness through cognitive and psychological mechanisms. Complex knowledge transformation served as the central cognitive mechanism, while self-efficacy functioned as a psychological process grounded in prior cognitive work.

## Journal of Computer Assisted Learning (2편)

### 1. Teacher Mediation and the Contingent Promise of Generative <scp>AI</scp> in Mathematics Education
- 저자: Allan M. Canonigo  |  게재: 2026-08-03
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70307](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70307)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background The integration of generative AI (GenAI) into education, specifically into teaching and learning, offers potential for personalised learning, but at the same time, it poses a challenge to whether it fosters student agency. Moreover, it also threatens to exacerbate entrenched inequalities, which have the potential to create new barriers for most of the learners. A gap exists in understanding how GenAI can be rethought as a dynamic scaffold that promotes cognitive struggle for conceptual understanding while actively advancing equitable participation. Objective The study aims to critically analyse how secondary school mathematics teachers implement GenAI for adaptive scaffolding. Moreover, this study aims to investigate the pedagogical and ethical tensions, particularly concerning equity and power dynamics, that emerge from using GenAI during classroom interactions. Methods This study employed a qualitative research design. Data were collected over an eight‐week intervention, which included in‐depth teacher interviews ( n = 10), student focus groups ( n = 24), and classroom observations in two public secondary schools purposively selected for their contrasting socioeconomic and technology integration profiles. Results Based on the results and findings, five distinct teacher‐mediated GenAI scaffolding practices that support equitable knowledge construction were identified. Findings indicate that strategies such as prompting for problem formulation through AI‐generated inquiry and facilitating critical comparison of AI‐generated outputs were associated with deeper student engagement and reasoning within the conditions of this study, particularly when teachers exercised intentional critical mediation. Conversely, unmediated use of GenAI amplified significant challenges, such as a redistribution of epistemic authority, a tangible “algorithmic divide” in AI performance between well‐resourced and under‐resourced schools, and risks due to the absence of how data should be handled. The technology consistently amplified existing pedagogical conditions, which are both effective and inequitable. Conclusion The promise of GenAI is strictly contingent on a commitment to equity. This means that its outcomes are determined not by the GenAI itself but by pedagogical intentionality and systemic support. Unexamined adoption poses risks that worsen disparities for marginalised students and reinforces passive learning. Thus, by proposing a framework for equitable integration centred on the teacher's role as a critical mediator, this expertise serves as a model for sustainable pedagogical practice in the digital age.

### 2. The Effects of K‐12 Artificial Intelligence Education in Enhancing <scp>AI</scp> Literacy: A Meta‐Analysis
- 저자: Wonjin Yu, Nari Kim, Ammi Chang, Wanju Huang  |  게재: 2026-08-03
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70308](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70308)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background The initiation of K‐12 artificial intelligence education has been led by numerous governments and institutions. However, most existing meta‐analyses have focused on the effectiveness of AI tools for learning support (AI in education; AIED), rather than AI education, and research on comprehensive evidence on how AI education improves learners' AI literacy remains limited. Objectives This meta‐analysis aims to provide an additional understanding of the future direction of K‐12 AI education by synthesising and analysing existing interventions in AI education classes to enhance learners' AI literacy and holistically discussing their effectiveness. Methods A total of 16 source articles were searched and analysed from 8 databases, considering our inclusion and exclusion criteria, yielding 57 effect sizes. These effect sizes were used to examine the effectiveness of various K‐12 AI education classes in enhancing AI literacy. Results and Conclusion The analysis showed a significant overall mean effect of AI education classes ( g = 0.892, CI: [0.548, 1.236], p &lt; 0.001) on enhancing AI literacy. Moreover, moderator analyses by publication type, year of publication, and school level of the participants did not reveal any significant patterns, indicating that K‐12 AI education classes exhibit consistently positive effects across different contexts. The findings underscore the importance of continued research on K‐12 AI education and offer a strong rationale for its ongoing integration by policymakers across various governments. To support discussion on this topic, future studies can reference this research and investigate the topic with more published articles.

## Journal of Research on Technology in Education (1편)

### 1. Unpacking the relationships among key factors in the effectiveness of inquiry-based learning: What changes does ChatGPT bring?
- 저자: Pin‐Hui Li, Kinshuk, Yueh‐Min Huang, Ting‐Ting Wu  |  게재: 2026-08-03
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2026.2711652](https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2026.2711652)

**초록:**
Previous studies on inquiry-based learning have seldom examined the structural relationships among curiosity, engagement, inquiry skills, and cognitive outcomes in generative AI environments. To address this gap, this study utilized ANCOVA and PLS-SEM with 462 Taiwanese high school students to compare traditional and ChatGPT-assisted IBL. Results indicated that while ChatGPT-assisted IBL significantly enhanced problem-solving skills, its impact on critical thinking and creativity was limited, as automated responses reduced opportunities for skepticism and independent verification. Furthermore, mediation analysis revealed that curiosity did not directly drive knowledge construction; its effects were fully mediated by learning engagement, underscoring engagement’s critical bridging role. Ultimately, this study clarifies how generative AI reshapes students’ learning pathways across distinct instructional settings, offering valuable empirical benchmarks for designing effective AI-supported learning scaffolds.
