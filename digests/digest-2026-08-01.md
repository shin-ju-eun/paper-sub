## International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education (1편)

### 1. Differential engagement with generative artificial intelligence in higher education: Gender, motivation, and achievement trajectories
- 저자: Yuan‐Hsuan Lee, Jiun‐Yu Wu  |  게재: 2026-07-30
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00615-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00615-8)

**초록:**
Abstract This study moves beyond asking whether generative AI (GenAI) improves learning to examine who engages, how they engage, and what kinds of questions they ask. We investigated how graduate students used a GenAI chatbot designed based on Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) architecture to support statistics learning and how engagement patterns associated with learner characteristics, motivation, and academic growth. Drawing on 2,819 chatbot queries across a semester, motivational measures, and performance data from 97 students, we modeled engagement using a zero-inflated negative binomial (ZINB) framework and identified performance gain profiles through k -means clustering. More than one-quarter of students did not use the chatbot at all, highlighting meaningful non-participation. Engagement frequency was shaped by learner differences: female students, those with stronger autonomous motivation, lower prior knowledge, and higher course performance interacted more frequently, while higher-performing students were less likely to abstain from use. Importantly, engagement was not merely a matter of frequency but of inquiry quality and diversity. Growing Achievers (students who began with weaker achievement but demonstrated substantial performance gains) posed more diverse and conceptually oriented questions. In contrast, Declining Performers engaged minimally and focused on narrower, procedural inquiries despite stronger initial knowledge. These findings suggest that GenAI tools function differently across learner subpopulations and can either amplify growth or remain underutilized depending on motivational and achievement trajectories. The observed variability in student engagement profiles indicates that the efficacy of AI integration depends on moving beyond uniform technological access toward strategies that prioritize autonomous motivation and scaffold concept-focused AI support tailored to individual learner trajectories. Such supports are essential to ensure shared human-AI agency so that AI integration expands learning opportunities and promotes upward academic mobility instead of concentrating benefits among already advantaged learners.

## Journal of Computer Assisted Learning (2편)

### 1. A Study on the Role of Midjourney, A Generative Artificial Intelligence Tool, in Enhancing Student Creativity in Illustration Design Classes
- 저자: Shuaisen He  |  게재: 2026-07-30
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70305](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70305)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Generative artificial intelligence text‐to‐image tools are increasingly used in design education, but evidence is limited on whether structured classroom integration improves expert‐rated creativity outcomes while controlling homogenisation. The objective was to test whether a Studio GenAI Creativity Enhancement Model (S GCEM) workflow using Midjourney improves expert‐rated illustration creativity versus a conventional non‐generative workflow and whether process metrics explain observed differences. Methods A parallel‐group, two‐section quasi‐experiment was conducted in an undergraduate illustration design studio. Students in the intervention section used S GCEM with Midjourney for early‐stage ideation only, mandatory prompt and version logs, and identical critique milestones to the control section (manual brainstorming, thumbnail sketching, non‐generative references). The analytic sample was n = 162 (S GCEM n = 82; control n = 80). Baseline and final artefacts were scored on 1–5 rubrics for originality, appropriateness, and elaboration. Ideation breadth and iteration depth were derived from logs. Analyses used analysis of covariance and regression. Results Final Product Creativity was higher under S GCEM than control after baseline adjustment, F(1,159) = 9.000, p = 0.003, partial η 2 = 0.053. Final Originality differed by condition, F(1,159) = 6.000, p = 0.015, partial η 2 = 0.036; Appropriateness, F(1,159) = 4.000, p = 0.047, partial η 2 = 0.024; and Elaboration, F(1,159) = 9.000, p = 0.003, partial η 2 = 0.054. Ideation breadth increased under S GCEM, B = 0.95, t = 4.13, p = 0.001, 95% CI 0.50–1.40, and iteration depth increased, B = 0.70, t = 3.50, p = 0.001, 95% CI 0.30–1.10. Homogenisation was lower under S GCEM, U = 2500, z = −3.05, p = 0.002, r = 0.24. Conclusion A structured Midjourney‐supported ideation workflow improved expert‐rated illustration creativity while increasing documented ideation activity and reducing homogenisation indicators.

### 2. Effects of Digital Technology on Creativity and Emotional Skills in Music Education: A Quantitative Study
- 저자: Ya Ni, Yingjin Shan  |  게재: 2026-07-30
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70303](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70303)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Digital technology has greatly transformed the traditional way of teaching music, particularly in the field of music creation. Students are less aware of using digital technology to gain creativity, skills and engagement. This study aims to evaluate the effects of digital technology on the creativity and emotions of students. Method A quantitative method was employed using an online questionnaire and responses were collected from 487 respondents. Data analysis through Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) helps to test Pearson correlation, Confirmatory Factor Analysis, path coefficient, one‐way ANOVA and two‐way ANOVA. Results The outcome reveals that there is a substantial positive correlation among the constructs in music education ( r = 0.505 to 0.786, p &lt; 0.01). Younger participants have experienced a higher impact on learning than older groups ( F = 89.619, η 2 = 0.360). Age and gender differences affect music education ( p &lt; 0.05), except for playing instruments ( p = 0.074). In this interaction, age shows a stronger and more reliable effect than gender, which is considered a main factor. Digital technology in music creates a substantial effect in learning style, personalised learning experiences, engagement, emotions and creativity ( β = 0.228 to 0.786). Conclusion Digital technology positively influences music education in terms of better engagement with students, the development of their skills and makes learning personal and collaborative. Adaptive platforms, virtual instruments and other tools facilitate cognitive, emotional and creative development. The effect of its influence on music coaching, especially on the psychological development and reflective practice of students, should be studied in future.
