## Interactive Learning Environments (1편)

### 1. Evaluating a staged interactive research-based model for perceived research competence in future educational managers: a randomized pretest-posttest study
- 저자: Zou Ruowei  |  게재: 2026-10-08
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2744900](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2744900)

**초록:**
Future educational managers require the capacity to formulate researchable problems, interpret educational data, and communicate evidence for managerial decisions. This study evaluated a 12-week staged, interactive research-based model designed to strengthen perceived research competence among master's students in educational management. The randomized pretest-posttest trial included 143 students from six universities in Kyiv (control, n = 72; experimental, n = 71). The intervention combined sequenced mini-research projects, collaborative work, mentoring, reflection, and the pedagogically structured use of Google Sheets, Microsoft Excel, SPSS Statistics 26.0, Tableau, and Power BI; the control condition received the same scheduled contact time through lectures, seminars, written tasks, and independent literature work. Perceived research competence was assessed with the 36-item PR-Comp scale. The findings support larger self-reported gains among study completers, but the complete-case analysis does not preserve the original randomized comparison for participants lost to follow-up and does not demonstrate objective research performance. The model's contribution lies in coordinating common research-based practices and digital tools into a replicable sequence tailored to evidence-informed educational management.

## Journal of Computer Assisted Learning (3편)

### 1. The Modality Principle in Immersive and Non‐Immersive Virtual Reality: An Eye Tracking Study
- 저자: Noé Monsaingeon, Stéphane Argon, Jean‐Michel Boucheix  |  게재: 2026-10-09
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70350](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70350)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background According to the modality principle of the Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning, learning is better facilitated when visual content is accompanied by a vocal narration compared to text explanation. However, the application of this principle in immersive virtual reality (IVR) remains unclear. Objectives The present experiment aimed to evaluate the effect of immersion on the application of the modality principle by comparing desktop virtual reality with immersive virtual reality. Methods A total of 105 seventh‐grade pupils, age from 12 to 13 years old, followed a 10‐min system‐paced lesson on forest development. The lesson was presented either in immersive virtual reality or in desktop VR, with limited and matched interactivity across media, and with verbal information delivered either as audio narration or on‐screen text. Learning outcomes were assessed across factual knowledge, conceptual knowledge, comprehension, and knowledge transfer, along with eye movements, presence, and cognitive load. Results and Conclusions Results indicate modulated modality effect on factual knowledge and improved conceptual learning on the desktop VR compared to IVR, which can be explained by fewer visual fixations on relevant areas. Students reported improved germane cognitive load in IVR when texts accompanied the illustrations. The modality principle does not seem to apply in the same way depending on the degree of immersion. The content of a virtual reality lesson should be adapted according to the type of knowledge being sought.

### 2. Ethical Generative AI Use in Higher Education: An Action Research Study Using the Think Aloud Method
- 저자: Suha Karaki, Sandra Baroudi  |  게재: 2026-10-09
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70352](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70352)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background As generative AI becomes increasingly embedded in higher education, it presents significant challenges related to ethical use, academic integrity and students' potential over‐reliance on AI‐generated outputs. The Think Aloud method offers a pedagogical approach for making students' reasoning visible as they interact with generative AI tools. Objective This mixed‐methods action research study explored the role of the Think Aloud method as a pedagogical intervention in supporting students' metacognitive awareness and ethical engagement with generative AI tools in a first‐year entrepreneurship course. Method Data were collected through reflective journals, classroom observations and a post‐intervention survey. The study involved 44 first‐year undergraduate students, with 38 students completing the post‐intervention survey. Qualitative data were analysed thematically, while survey responses were examined using descriptive statistics and an exploratory correlation analysis. Results and Conclusions Findings suggested that Think Aloud supported students' emerging awareness of prompt construction, verification practices and ethical decision‐making when using generative AI. However, students' critical evaluation of AI‐generated content often remained at a surface level, particularly among students requiring additional support. The study indicates that Think Aloud can make students' AI‐related reasoning more visible and can support more intentional engagement with generative AI, but it should not be viewed as a stand‐alone solution. Rather, it appears most effective when embedded within a broader pedagogical framework that includes AI literacy, prompt engineering, verification strategies and evaluative judgement. The study contributes classroom‐based evidence on process‐oriented approaches to ethical generative AI use in higher education and highlights the need for sustained instructional scaffolding to support deeper critical engagement with AI tools.

### 3. Retrieval‐Augmented Lesson Generation With Large Language Models for Scenario‐Based Tutor Training
- 저자: Jionghao Lin, Sandy Yiyang Zhao, Ashish Gurung, xianghui meng, Kenneth R. Koedinger  |  게재: 2026-10-09
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70343](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70343)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background High‐impact tutoring can improve student learning, but scaling tutoring programmes requires large numbers of novice tutors. Developing high‐quality tutor‐training lessons remains a key bottleneck. Objectives This study compared post‐test performance after participants studied human‐created or LLM‐created tutor‐training materials and examined whether immediate explanatory feedback moderated differences by lesson creation source (human‐created vs. LLM‐created). Methods We conducted a randomized eight‐condition between‐subjects experiment. Participants completed a source‐matched pre‐instruction practice assessment, studied a tutor‐training lesson created by the same source, and then completed two fixed‐order post‐lesson assessments. The design compared lesson creation source, manipulated immediate explanatory feedback and counterbalanced the pre‐instruction scenario form. Post‐test performance was the mean proportion correct across the human‐created and LLM‐created post‐lesson assessments. Results and Conclusions Average post‐test performance was higher after the human‐created lesson than after the LLM‐created lesson, but this difference depended on feedback. The human‐created advantage appeared in the no‐feedback condition, whereas no statistically reliable lesson creation source difference was detected when feedback was provided. Feedback had no statistically significant overall main effect. The human‐created lesson condition performed better on the human‐created post‐lesson assessment, while the LLM‐created post‐lesson assessment showed a small descriptive difference favouring the LLM‐created lesson condition. Because pre‐instruction performance differed by lesson creation source and scenario form, and because assessment source was confounded with fixed assessment order, the results do not isolate pure learning gains or establish a general transfer advantage.
