### 1. One Prompt Does Not Fit All: Comparing Uniform and Adaptive Code‐Specific Prompting for Deductive Qualitative Coding With Large Language Models
- 저자: María Jesús Rodríguez‐Triana, Luis P. Prieto, Mohamed Saban, Juan Ignacio Asensio-Pérez, Cristina Villa-Torrano et al.  |  게재: 2026-09-24
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70334](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70334)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated potential for (semi‐)automating the qualitative analysis of unstructured data, particularly in deductive qualitative coding using codebooks. While prior research has shown the feasibility of this technology, model performance seems to vary depending on the nature of the constructs being coded. However, existing approaches typically apply a single LLM prompting strategy across entire datasets, often of discourse transcripts or questionnaire data, using a single coding scheme or theoretical frame. Objectives We aim to explore whether code‐specific adaptive prompting, where LLM prompts are customised based on expert coders' or data‐driven rules for specific codes, outperforms uniform prompting in terms of agreement with a negotiated human coding and, secondarily, reduced computational cost when agreement is maintained. Methods We apply this approach to analyse the description of 35 multimedia learning designs (comprising 758 items/activities) created by teachers in an inquiry‐based learning digital platform. Two human coders and two open‐weights LLMs (Llama 3.3 and GPT‐OSS) coded the dataset, attending to three different pedagogical frameworks. Results and Conclusions Our results indicate that, with both LLMs, the data‐driven adaptive prompting reached similar or higher agreement with the reference point (i.e., the coding agreed by humans) than uniform prompting strategies (namely, zero‐shot and few‐shot with and without context), while reducing costs. These results may be especially relevant for the coding of large‐scale datasets, highlighting opportunities for human–AI collaboration in deductive qualitative coding of educational data.

### 2. Lifelong, Place‐Based and Gameful Design Perceptions on Virtual Reality Immersive Learning Experiences
- 저자: Robson Araujo, Alec M. Bodzin  |  게재: 2026-09-24
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70335](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70335)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background There is a need to better understand how immersive technologies, specifically virtual reality (VR), can support learning across the lifespan and in different learning settings. Prior work emphasizes the importance of empirical investigations that include diverse learner characteristics, examine how these diverse elements relate to learning outcomes, and identify mechanisms that may enable or constrain effective learning. Scholars also advocate for interdisciplinary approaches that integrate immersive technologies and thoughtful design to support meaningful learning experiences. Objectives This study examines how learners' perceptions of the immersive learning experience with Watershed Explorers: Environmental Issues in desktop VR (dVR) and headset VR (hVR) are associated with their reported engagement and motivation across formal, non‐formal, and informal learning settings and a wide age range. Methods Data were collected over 6 months using convenience sampling. The dataset included 151 participants aged 8–90 across formal, non‐formal, and informal learning settings. The Cognitive Affective Model of Immersive Learning (CAMIL) informed construct selection, and the Technology‐enhanced Lifelong Learning Ecology (TeLLE) conceptual framework guided the regression approach. Analyses examined how learners' perceived VR affordances (immersion and presence), perceived gameful design features (narrative, tutorial, and agency), perceived local context (place‐based content), as well as learning settings (formal, non‐formal, informal), device (dVR or hVR), and age were associated with their reported affective outcomes (engagement and motivation). Survey‐mode differences (i.e., embedded survey for dVR; paper survey for hVR) align with device conditions and represent a limitation for interpreting device comparisons. Results and Conclusions Participants across learning settings and the sampled age range reported high perceived immersion and presence. Perceived gameful design showed the strongest associations with self‐reported affective outcomes, followed by perceived VR affordances and perceived local context. Age showed a small but statistically significant positive association with affective outcomes, suggesting that lifespan factors contribute meaningfully alongside design perceptions. Perceived affective outcomes mean scores were similar for dVR and hVR, which showed negligible associations with outcomes; however, caution is advised when interpreting device differences due to survey‐mode alignment. Overall, results suggest that learners' perceptions of design quality, rather than device type, are more strongly associated with their reported motivation and engagement in immersive VR experiences across learning settings.

### 3. Students' Perceptions of University Preparation and Perceived Industry Relevance of AI Skills: A Study in UAE Higher Education
- 저자: Zeina Hojeij, Fahed Bizzari  |  게재: 2026-09-24
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70342](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70342)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Artificial intelligence is changing how universities teach and how graduates prepare for work. While universities are expected to support students in developing AI‐related knowledge and confidence, less is known about how students themselves perceive the relevance of this preparation to the industry needs they anticipate. Objectives This study explores students' perceptions of how well their institutions prepare them for AI‐related workplace challenges, how relevant they perceive their AI education to be to industry expectations, and which learning experiences they see as most useful for developing valued AI competencies. Methods The study used a survey design with open‐ended elaboration, involving 114 students (86% at a single UAE university, with a small number from other UAE and non‐UAE institutions). The survey included Likert‐scale, multiple‐choice, and open‐ended items. Data were analyzed descriptively, with gender and year‐of‐study comparisons tested using Mann–Whitney U and Kruskal‐Wallis tests; open‐ended responses were analyzed thematically. Results and Conclusions Students strongly believed universities should prepare all learners for AI‐shaped workplaces but reported only moderate confidence in current preparation, with no significant differences by gender or year of study. Many described a gap between theoretical learning and practical application and wanted current tools, career guidance, and ethical use. Students valued hands‐on projects, industry partnerships, internships, curriculum integration, and responsible AI education. These findings reflect student perceptions rather than verified industry requirements and suggest universities need more visible, applied, industry‐connected AI education alongside continued attention to ethics and critical thinking.

### 4. Investigating Synchronous Interaction Behaviours Among Open University Students: A Latent Profile Analysis and Epistemic Network Analysis
- 저자: Jin Zhu, Liu Jinglu, Ran Wang  |  게재: 2026-09-24
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70339](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70339)

**초록:**
ABSTRACT Background Online education plays a pivotal role in contemporary higher education systems, particularly for distance learners at open universities, who often face challenges such as low learner engagement and perceived social isolation. However, the heterogeneity of interaction behaviours among open university students—a population whose learning ecology is shaped by work‐study conflicts and constrained synchronous availability—remains critically underexplored. Objectives This study aimed to investigate synchronous and asynchronous interaction behaviours among open university students, identify distinct interaction profiles, and provide evidence‐based, profile‐specific insights for differentiated instructional design. Methods A mixed‐methods approach was employed, combining latent profile analysis (LPA) and epistemic network analysis (ENA). Unlike prior studies, this study uses LPA to identify interaction profiles grounded in theoretically derived dimensions of teacher‐student and student–student interactions across synchronous and asynchronous modalities, and ENA to uncover the cognitive discourse structures underlying those profiles, thereby bridging self‐reported behavioural frequencies with actual epistemic engagement. Data were collected from 372 students at a Chinese open university over 12 weeks. Results Three distinct interaction profiles emerged: (1) Teacher‐student interaction enthusiasts (TIE): preferred real‐time interactions with instructors; (2) selective asynchronous collaborators (SAC): Balanced synchronous and asynchronous peer collaboration; and (3) high interaction achievers (HIA): Actively engaged in all interaction types. ENA revealed varying cognitive and social engagement patterns across profiles. One‐way ANOVA confirmed significant performance differences among the three groups ( F (2, 369) = 107.14, p < 0.001, η 2 = 0.367), with HIA > SAC > TIE. Conclusions The findings underscore the importance of learner‐centric instructional design and provide actionable profile‐specific strategies: scaffolded real‐time responses for TIE, flexible blended designs for SAC, and leadership roles for HIA. These evidence‐based profiles advocate for inclusive digital education models that accommodate cognitive diversity in open university settings.
