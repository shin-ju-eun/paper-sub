## International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education (1편)

### 1. Evolving role of educators in higher education: an interview study on three perspectives
- 저자: Adrijana Krebs, Lana Ivanjek, Alexander Steinmaurer  |  게재: 2026-11-26
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00642-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00642-5)

**초록:**
Existing research on AI in higher education has emphasized digital competencies and technology integration, however, less attention has been paid to how changing educator roles are understood across stakeholder groups and shaped by institutional conditions. This qualitative study examines how educators, students, and educational developers construct the evolving role of educators in response to educational technologies, including generative AI, and how these changes are shaped by emotional responses and institutional conditions. Drawing on semi-structured interviews with 20 participants, the study employs inductive reflexive thematic analysis within a contextualist, interpretive framework, using interactionist role theory and, to a lesser extent, control-value theory to interpret role changes and emotional responses to technology, respectively. The findings indicate that the educator role is being renegotiated rather than replaced. Technology was predominantly framed as assistance rather than substitution, while educators remained positioned as responsible for pedagogical judgment, ethical oversight, and human guidance. Emotional responses to technology were mixed and often shaped by uncertainty, trust, and perceived control. Role adaptation was also described as strongly mediated by institutional conditions, including workload, large classes, research–teaching tensions, and uneven engagement with professional development. The paper contributes by shifting attention from technology adoption alone to the interactional, emotional, and institutional processes through which the educator’s role is being reworked in higher education.

## Interactive Learning Environments (2편)

### 1. Generative AI-supported learning in vocational automotive education: effects on learning effectiveness, motivation, and multimodal communication competence
- 저자: Cathy Weng, Kifle Kassaw, Chen Chia-le, Apollo Weng, Tsai-Ju Lee  |  게재: 2026-11-25
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2767865](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2767865)

**초록:**
Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) is increasingly used in education, yet empirical evidence of its effectiveness in vocational secondary education remains limited. This study examined the effects of GenAI-supported learning on learning effectiveness, motivation, and multimodal communication competence among 59 second-year vocational high school students enrolled in an automotive course in Taiwan. Using a quasi-experimental design during the 2023–2024 academic year, the experimental group engaged with GenAI tools for text and image generation, while the control group used conventional digital learning resources. Data were collected through pre- and post-tests, an adapted Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire (MSLQ), and a rubric-based assessment. The results showed that the experimental group significantly outperformed the control group in learning effectiveness. Both groups demonstrated significant improvements in learning motivation, with no significant between-group differences. However, the control group achieved higher multimodal communication competence scores, suggesting a possible mismatch between GenAI-supported activities and text-oriented assessment criteria. These findings provide preliminary evidence of the potential cognitive benefits of GenAI-supported learning in vocational education, while highlighting the importance of aligning instructional activities with assessment approaches.

### 2. The erosion of necessity pressure: how generative AI reshapes the conditions of cognitive engagement in higher education
- 저자: Muhammad Hashier Muneeb Farrukh, Arkhangelskaya Maria, Ali Raza, Issam A. Ghazzawi  |  게재: 2026-11-26
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2766316](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2766316)

**초록:**
This study examines how continuous access to generative artificial intelligence reshapes cognitive engagement in higher education. Rather than focusing only on learning outcomes, efficiency, or cognitive offloading, the study investigates how persistent AI availability may influence the conditions under which students perceive continued independent thinking as necessary. Drawing on a qualitative, theory-building study of 25 undergraduate students in Shenzhen, China, the study collected data through 18 face-to-face interviews and 7 asynchronous written interviews with students who regularly use generative AI in their academic work. The findings develop the concept of epistemic insurance, referring to the expectation that unresolved difficulty can be externally resolved at relatively low cost. This condition can weaken necessity pressure, making sustained cognitive engagement more conditional and allowing task completion to outpace independent understanding in some situations. However, this mechanism is not deterministic. Students who deliberately delay, restrict, or selectively use AI can preserve greater independent engagement, identifying self-binding as an important boundary condition. The study contributes a process-level explanation of how the continuous availability of generative AI may influence when students persist with difficulty, when they externalize cognitive effort, and how this shapes the relationship between task completion and independent understanding.

### 3. From learning analytics engagement indicators to learning pathways: Examining motivation and instructional context in a K-12 science PBL program
- 저자: Songhee Han, Jueun Shin, Jiyoon Han, Ying Cai, Min Liu  |  게재: 2026-11-26
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-026-14192-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-026-14192-w)

**초록:**
L​earning analytics research has provided valuable evidence about students’ engagement in digital environments. However, little is known about the relationships among engagement indicators, activity-specific motivation, teacher-shaped instructional context, and temporal engagement pathways in K-12 science PBL. This study addressed that gap by examining how engagement indicators functioned in a K-12 science PBL program. Data were drawn from 253 middle school students at one school participating in a three-week digital science PBL program across three teacher-shaped instructional contexts. The data included system log-based engagement indicators, note-level interaction records, and post-activity motivation survey responses. Four frequency-based engagement indicators were analyzed: note-taking, solution attempts, successful solutions, and incorrect solutions. The primary multiple regression analysis showed that note-taking was most strongly and positively associated with motivation, whereas solution-related indicators were not significantly associated with motivation in that model. Moderation analyses did not detect statistically significant differences across instructional contexts in the associations between engagement indicators and motivation. However, between-group comparisons showed differences in solution-related behaviors across teacher-shaped instructional contexts, and Markov chain modeling of note-level records showed that engagement pathways differed across these contexts in their concentration and dispersion. These findings highlight the value of considering note-taking, instructional context, and temporal pathway structure when examining student engagement in K-12 science PBL.
