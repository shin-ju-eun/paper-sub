## International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education (1편)

### 1. Competency‑based and emotional predictors of pre‑service teachers’ orientation towards the responsible use of Generative AI
- 저자: Ana María Pinto-Llorente, Óscar José Martín Sánchez, María Teresa Del Moral Marcos  |  게재: 2026-11-24
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00643-4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00643-4)

**초록:**
The integration of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) into initial teacher education requires an understanding not only of how frequently these tools are used, but also of the factors that shape their responsible adoption. This study examines how pre‑service teachers’ orientation towards responsible GenAI use is configured, identifying the competency-based resources and usage‑related variables that underpin it, as well as the role of anxiety across the dimensions assessed. A total of 298 pre‑service teachers from two Spanish universities participated, completing a validated 25‑item instrument comprising five dimensions: attitudes towards the use of AI for employability, digital flow in the responsible and safe use of AI, creativity to use AI responsibly, anxiety about using AI in future work contexts, and responsible use of AI for employability. Robust group comparisons, hierarchical regressions, and a network analysis with hierarchical clustering were conducted. The confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) supported the instrument’s five‑factor structure. Creativity (β = 0.42) and digital flow (β = 0.37) emerged as the strongest predictors of responsible use, whereas usage frequency made no unique positive contribution. Anxiety was higher among women and younger participants; in the regression model it was associated with more favourable attitudes and higher digital flow, and with lower creativity only when these were controlled (a suppression effect). It occupied a peripheral position within the correlation network. The findings are synthesised in an exploratory two‑path empirical model that requires confirmatory testing. Initial teacher education may therefore benefit from fostering creative GenAI‑based experiences while deliberately addressing the emotional dimension of learning these technologies.

### 1. Co-designing inclusive hybrid learning environments: an empirical validation of the CHIVE framework for immersive virtual reality in higher education
- 저자: Mohamed Oubibi, Atif Saleem, Antony Zakaria Fute  |  게재: 2026-11-24
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2767140](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2767140)

**초록:**
Background and Aim Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) globally are striving to develop inclusive, flexible, and immersive learning environments that accommodate diverse student populations, including those with disabilities. This study introduces CHIVE (Co-design, Hybrid Integration, Immersive VR, and Equity), an empirically tested institutional framework integrating these dimensions.Method A mixed-methods design was employed, featuring a validated 40-item self-report survey completed by 405 participants (320 students and 85 faculty members) and participatory co-design workshops involving 48 students and 18 faculty members. The survey assessed perceived VR usability, engagement quality, accessibility satisfaction, co-design participation, and inclusive learning experience. Students who self-identified as having a disability or learning difference constituted 38% of the student sample, while first-generation university students represented 29%. Quantitative data were analyzed using confirmatory factor analysis and structural equation modeling, and qualitative workshop data were analyzed using thematic analysis.Findings Co-design participation emerged as the strongest predictor of self-reported inclusive learning experience (β = 0.61, p < .001). Perceived VR usability was positively associated with engagement quality (β = 0.47, p < .001) and accessibility satisfaction (β = 0.39, p < .001). Students who self-identified as having a disability or learning difference reported lower accessibility satisfaction than non-disabled students (M = 2.94 vs. M = 3.54), while disabled workshop participants reported higher accessibility satisfaction than disabled survey-only respondents (M = 3.42 vs. M = 2.61). These differences should be interpreted as associations because workshop participation was voluntary rather than randomly assigned. Qualitative analysis identified four themes: participatory ownership, sensory and navigational accessibility, pedagogical coherence, and institutional readiness.Discussion CHIVE extends CAMIL through co-design and institutional belonging, advancing UDL into immersive hybrid learning while highlighting persistent accessibility barriers.Conclusion CHIVE provides an evidence-based framework emphasizing institutional readiness, faculty development, and accessibility audits..

### 2. Cognitive engagement of early childhood learners with AI avatars as learning companions in the metaverse: an ICAP-Based analysis
- 저자: Manuel B. Garcia  |  게재: 2026-11-24
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2755543](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2755543)

**초록:**
As digital technologies become increasingly embedded in early childhood education (ECE), understanding their developmental implications has become more urgent. Although systematic reviews have examined emerging technologies in ECE, the convergence of artificial intelligence (AI) and metaverse remains underexplored. Furthermore, existing studies have rarely examined how relational and embodied digital interactions contribute to deeper forms of learning among young children. Therefore, this study investigated the cognitive engagement of preschoolers interacting with AI-powered avatars within a developmentally appropriate metaverse environment. Using a qualitative-dominant mixed methods design, 30 preschool-aged children completed three dialogic learning sessions, with interaction transcripts, behavioral observations, system logs, and field notes analyzed through ICAP-based coding and supporting quantitative comparisons. Findings revealed that active engagement was the dominant mode, but over time, participation increasingly shifted toward constructive and interactive forms. This progression was supported by the constellation of spatial interactivity, narrative immersion, and embodied presence afforded by the metaverse, alongside the relational adaptivity of AI avatars through memory-based dialogue. These results highlight the potential of AI-driven metaverse to extend early learning by promoting collaborative meaning-making and sustained dialogic participation. Implications for the intentional design of relational AI systems and the development of human-centered metaverse learning environments in ECE are discussed.
