## International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education (1편)

### 1. Understanding the role of learning analytics and generative artificial intelligence on decision-making and learning design practice in higher education
- 저자: Alrike Claassen, Daniel Ebbert, Vitomir Kovanović, Negin Mirriahi, Shane Dawson  |  게재: 2026-08-24
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00619-4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00619-4)

**초록:**
Abstract High-quality learning design is an important contributor to student success. Higher education institutions can leverage the combined potential of learning analytics (LA) and Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) to enhance instructors’ learning design practices. However, the way LA and GenAI are being used in combination for learning design is not well understood. In this context, one theory that can provide valuable insights into instructors’ technology integration into learning design (LD) practice is Self-Determination Theory (SDT). Using SDT as an interpretative lens, this study illuminates the factors in instructors’ learning design practices when utilising LA and GenAI. For this purpose, we employed quantitative ethnography to explore how instructors utilise LA and GenAI for LD. Eleven focus groups were conducted with instructors from a large Australian university. Using thematic analysis, we created a codebook consisting of ten codes that were applied to our dataset and then analysed using Epistemic Network Analysis (ENA). Our results show that instructors’ LA-informed LD discussions primarily revolved around course, contextual, and creative problem-solving considerations, as well as student support. The predominant GenAI factors were assessment design, contextual consideration, creative problem solving and design for student self-determination. Based on our results, we discuss the integration of LA and GenAI in LD practice and decision-making, highlighting the practical implications of our findings.

### 2. Designing a scaffolded GenAI revision environment for EMI academic writing: a randomized active-control study of no-ai transfer and feedback use
- 저자: Stanley Y. B. Huang  |  게재: 2026-08-24
- 링크: [https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2713874](https://doi.org/10.1080/10494820.2026.2713874)

**초록:**
Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) can improve assisted drafts without demonstrating students’ ability to write independently. This randomized active-control study compared standard GenAI with a scaffolded revision environment integrating dialogic prompting, bounded Mandarin clarification, and a reflective revision loop in a Taiwanese English-medium instruction (EMI) writing module. The complete-case sample comprised 428 undergraduates. One week after revision, the scaffolded group achieved a higher score on a writing task without AI (75.86 vs. 72.99). Observed-score path analysis using standardized variables and a 0/1 condition indicator showed that the scaffolded condition was associated with post-task need support (β = .393, p < .001). Need support was associated with a higher coder-derived feedback-use index (β = .376, p < .001), which was associated with higher no-AI transfer-writing scores (β = .324, p < .001). Condition retained a direct association with transfer writing (β = .203, p = .007), while the theory-ordered serial indirect association was .048 (95% bootstrap CI [.026, .076]). Because need support was assessed after revision, the indirect pathway is interpreted as a model-based association rather than causal mediation. Thus, the randomized difference in writing after AI removal provides the strongest evidence, while the process model offers a provisional explanation.
